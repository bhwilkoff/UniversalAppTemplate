Privacy

Your work is yours, and we keep very little.

This is everything the site keeps about the people who use it, written so that you can check it against the code, which is public.

Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.

What I want for your information.

I want this site to know as little about you as it can while still letting a cohort learn together, so the rule I build by is that your work stays in your own GitHub account and the hub keeps only what a cohort needs in order to run. This page says exactly what that is, who can see each part, and when it goes away. It covers humanshaped.org, its hub (sign-in, cohorts, and live sessions), and the Human Shaped add-on for Google Meet.

Nothing here is sold, used for advertising, or used to train an AI model, and the site has no analytics, no tracking pixels, and no advertising cookies.

What comes from GitHub when you sign in.

Signing in is done with your GitHub account. From GitHub, the hub keeps your GitHub username, its number, your display name, and the address of your profile picture, and these are what other people in your cohort see. Supabase, the service that runs our sign-in and database, also keeps the email address GitHub gives it, which no page on this site shows to anyone.

GitHub also gives your browser a token so that pages like your cohort page can read and post to your cohort's discussions as you. That token is kept only in your browser, for as long as the tab is open (in what browsers call session storage), it is sent only to GitHub, the pages stop using it after seven and a half hours, and it is never saved in our database. You can take away the site's access to your GitHub account at any time in GitHub's settings, under Applications.

What the hub keeps, and who can see it.

Everything below is stored in a Supabase database in the United States, and the rules about who can read each part are enforced by the database itself rather than by the pages.

  • Which cohorts you are in, your role, and, if you add them, your app's name, repository, and address. Everyone in the cohort can see these.
  • Your group, and where your group is in its turns during a live session. Everyone in the cohort can see these.
  • What you choose to share with a cohort (a bring-back, a link, a question, or a request for feedback), and the feedback people write on it. Everyone in the cohort can see these.
  • An email address for calendar invitations, only if you give one, for one cohort. Only you and that cohort's teachers can see it.
  • Your answers to a check for understanding during a live session. Only you and your teachers see them, a teacher may show the room an anonymous count, and they are never graded.
  • Notes your teachers send you. Only you and the cohort's teachers can read them, the teacher who wrote one can correct its words (and you will see that it was edited), and you can remove any of them.
  • The session's shared drawing board and its "show your work" queue, which everyone in the cohort can see.
  • Whether you have been added to the cohort's private conversation on GitHub, which only you and your teachers can see.
  • Your credential, if you earn one at the end of a cohort. Only you and your teachers can see its record here, and its public page shows it only to someone who has its link.
  • A request to teach, if you make one, which only you and the people who decide on requests can read.
  • Your own marks on the path: the steps you marked done, whether you marked a stage ready or not yet, and any note you kept of what you would bring back. Only you can read them (and your own AI agent, if you connect one), and nothing counts or compares them. If you mark stages while signed out, the marks stay in that browser alone, and they move into your account the next time you sign in there.
  • How your teachers may reach you, only if you choose it on your account page: an address, whether your teachers may email you from their own email (then the teachers of cohorts you are in can see that address), and whether you would like a short email from meet@humanshaped.org when a teacher writes you a note or someone answers what you shared. That email says only that something is waiting, never the words. No AI agent can read the address, and you can turn either choice off or forget the address at any time.

Your app appears on the public apps page only if you switch it on yourself, and a teacher can keep an app off that page without changing your choice. Your code, your commits, and your app are never copied here: pages read them live from GitHub while you look.

When it goes away.

When a cohort is marked finished, its live-session queue, checks and answers, signals, drawing boards, and room turns are deleted. Notes from your teachers stay, because they are yours, until you remove them.

When you leave a cohort, what you shared there (and the feedback on it), your place in its queue, your answers, your group, your calendar email, and your app's details are deleted. Notes from your teachers stay on your account page until you remove them, and feedback you wrote on someone else's work stays with their work.

When you delete your account on your account page, everything the hub holds about you is deleted with it, including your notes, your answers, what you shared, the feedback you wrote, and your credential's record. A drawing you added to a shared board stays on that board, without your name, until the cohort finishes.

If you connect your own AI agent.

You can connect your own Claude or Gemini to the hub so that it can read your cohort's work with you. It asks for your permission on our consent page first, it can only read (it can never write, post, join, leave, or delete anything), and it sees only what you can see. What it reads then goes to the company that runs your agent, under your own account with them, so their privacy policy applies to that part.

Google Meet, and the add-on.

Live sessions happen on Google Meet, and Google's own privacy policy covers the call. The Human Shaped add-on for Meet asks Google for no permissions, and the only thing it reads from Meet is the call's meeting code, which it uses to find the cohort or group the call belongs to. It does not record, transcribe, measure who talks, or join the call as a participant, and it never reads your Google account, your email, your contacts, or your files.

The hub never records a session. A host who chooses to record does so with their own recording tool, after saying so out loud and in the chat, and that recording is kept in the cohort's Google Drive folder, which only the cohort can open. The host may mark moments to make short clips, which go only to the cohort too. The host may also have their recorder count how much they themselves talk compared with everyone else, from the sound alone, never who else talks or what anyone says; if they choose, they save that one number, their own share, on the session page, where the cohort can read it until the cohort is finished. When a host sends calendar invitations, they go through Google Calendar from the host's account, to the email you gave for that purpose.

The services this site uses.

The site itself is served by GitHub Pages, the hub runs on Supabase, sign-in and conversations use GitHub, and sessions use Google Meet. Pages load the Commissioner typeface from Google Fonts and a few open-source scripts from cdnjs and jsDelivr, which, like any website, see your browser's address when it asks for them. Each of these has its own privacy policy, and none of them receives anything from us beyond what this page describes.

Your browser also keeps a few things on your own device: your sign-in, your choice of light or dark, short-lived copies of what GitHub returned (so a page does not ask twice), and, for teachers, unsent drafts of follow-ups, which never leave the teacher's browser.

Questions, and changes to this page.

If you have a question, or you want something removed that you cannot remove yourself, write to meet@humanshaped.org or ask in the discussions on GitHub. If this page changes, the change will be dated here and visible in the site's public history on GitHub.
