Build software for the parts of life only people can do.

Human Shaped is a free, five-week way to build an app of your own with an AI agent, alongside other people doing the same, without handing over the parts of the work where you actually learn. I built four apps this way before asking anyone else to, and I want it to be open to anyone with a problem worth solving.

The idea

Some problems were never computer-shaped.

In March, I typed a page about a post I had read on Bluesky, which said that we are only looking to solve "computer-shaped problems." A computer-shaped problem is one you can hand to a machine and be done with. But, learning, community, and the people we love are not like that, because they only happen when a person does the work.

Human-shaped software takes the mechanical work off people's hands and leaves them the learning. Here is the difference in one of my own apps.

Computer-shaped

A model writes a blurb for each of ten thousand films in an afternoon, and most folks would never know.

Human-shaped

Archive Watch pulls each film's notes from the people who uploaded and reviewed it on archive.org, which is slower, and the words stay theirs.

The phrase "computer-shaped problems" comes from a post on Bluesky that started all of this, and the longer version says what it means for the software you build.

The method

Three moves, every time you build.

An agent will happily write most of your code, and yet there are three things it cannot do for you, which is exactly why every round of building comes back to them.

  1. 1

    Write it down

    Writing is how you find out what you believe, and a value you have not written down is one the agent building your app has no way to keep.

  2. 2

    Prove it

    Proving is how you find out whether it is true, so every "it works" points at something a person can look at on a real device, rather than the agent's word for it.

  3. 3

    Live with it

    Living with the app is how you find out what you missed, because the values you could not have predicted only show up when you use it every day, with the people it is for.

Cohorts

Five weeks, with other people, for free.

A cohort runs for about five weeks, with one live session each week on Google Meet, and every person in it builds one real app of their own for people they can actually name. Every session is recorded, so missing a week never means falling behind.

  1. Your app lives on GitHub

    You build in your own repository, with your own AI agent, and it stays yours after the cohort ends.

  2. It gets a page here

    The hub reads your progress from GitHub with your permission, so the cohort can follow your app as it grows.

  3. Your cohort talks it through

    Classmates, partners, and your teacher see your work in progress and talk about it on GitHub, in the open or just among yourselves.

I want cohorts to stay free, and in time the people who finish one will be able to lead the next, which is how a method like this gets to more people than one teacher ever could.

The first cohort's dates will be posted on the cohorts page, which also says how a cohort works from the first week to the last.

Built this way

Four apps came before the method.

Before I asked anyone to build like this, I built four apps this way myself, and the template you will start from is what they taught me.

  • Archive Watch

    "A cinematheque for the Internet Archive. Free, public domain, no account."

    Started April 17, 2026, and now on Apple TV, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android, Google TV, Fire TV, Fire tablets, Roku, and the web, with 4,306 commits by the end of September.

    It gave the template most of what it knows about shared data, TV platforms, and testing on real devices, and the rule that only essential words belong on screen.

    • archivewatch.org

  • Tidbits Trivia

    "Trivia from the whole of Wikipedia. Play solo, keep a daily streak, or pass the phone."

    First built June 16, 2026, and now on the web, Apple's platforms, Android, and Windows through the Microsoft Store.

    It taught the template how to play together across platforms, and the rule that a computer opponent is always labeled as one.

    • tidbitstrivia.com

  • Bsky Dreams

    "Built for people who want to think more, not less."

    A way to read Bluesky, on the web and the iPhone.

    It gave the template a feed ranked by the reader's own values rather than by engagement, and a reader mode for the links people share.

    • bskydreams.com

  • BOBA Playbook

    "The definitive companion app for the Bo Jackson Battle Arena trading card game."

    On the web and Android, and no longer in active development.

    It is where the four questions every feature has to answer came from, and it stands as the proof that this method holds for large databases, huge numbers of files, camera scanning, 3D on iOS, and pricing.

    • bobaplaybook.com
