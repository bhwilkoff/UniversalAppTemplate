Apps that wear the builder's values on their sleeves.

The builder writes down what they value, where people and AI agents can both read it, and then makes something that lives by it. AI is a tool in the builder's hands, and never a replacement for the people involved.

Read the principles Why build this way

Human-shaped software:

  1. Starts from a human-shaped problem
  2. Increases what people are able to do
  3. Has its values written down as guardrails
  4. Treats iteration as the path to innovation
  5. Is grounded in research
  6. Makes every decision with feedback
  7. Is tested the way people will actually see, hear, and use it
  8. Treats documentation as seriously as code
  9. Shares its code, tools, and lessons
  10. Counts learning as success
  11. Lets people own their data
  12. Creates no new human-shaped problems
  13. Is written in the builder's own voice
  14. Credits everyone and everything responsible for it
  15. Brings joy to at least one person

The idea

Some problems were never computer-shaped.

Nilay Patel calls it software brain: seeing the whole world as databases waiting for the right code. AI has turned that habit up as far as it will go, but learning, community, friendship, and the people we love only happen when a person does the work.

Software brain

AI can write ten thousand film summaries in an afternoon, and not one of them comes from someone who watched the film.

Human-shaped

Notes from the people who watched each film are slower and sometimes need correcting, and they keep the people who loved those films in the story.

Humanity is inefficient, and that is the point.

Learning is inefficient too. We have great tools for making it better, and our values decide where those tools belong. Why build this way

The method

Write, play, publish.

Every round of building makes the same three moves, and the principles decide what each round is for.

  1. Write

    Writing gets it out of your head.

    Values, features, and feedback go where you and your agent can both read them.

  2. Play

    Playing makes your thinking tangible.

    You use what came back on a real device, and you decide whether it works.

  3. Publish

    Publishing lets other people play too.

    A human-shaped problem is rarely yours alone, and the people who use your work tell you what to write next.
