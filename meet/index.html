Each session happens inside Google Meet.

A cohort meets once a week on a Google Meet™ call, and the Human Shaped add-on puts that session's plan, its clock, and its questions in Meet's own side panel, so the people in the call stay the thing everyone is looking at and nobody has to keep a second page open beside it.

Add it to Google Meet

Free, from the Google Workspace Marketplace™. You add it once, with the Google account you use for Meet.

What it does while you meet.

The panel knows which cohort and which session the call belongs to from the call's own link, and a leader can run any session they lead in any call, to try out a draft or run a session again for someone who missed it. A leader sees the part of the session that is happening now and its clock, who is ready to show their work and in what order, the questions they prepared, and what people wrote before the session, and can put one thing at a time on the main stage for everyone: a welcome as people arrive, a question with its count of answers, someone's app while they talk about it, several people invited to speak, one chosen answer, or a breakout room's work.

A learner's panel follows the same session. It shows where the call is in the plan and what each part is for, it is where you answer a question, counted without names, and in a breakout room it holds your room's shared workbook and how long the room has left. Your leader may put one written answer on everyone's stage, with your name only if they choose to show it.

Why the session lives in the call.

The heart of every session is people showing what they built that week and talking through the decisions they made, so the tools should stay out of the way of that conversation. Everything a session needs is prepared beforehand on its page on humanshaped.org, and once the call starts, the add-on carries that preparation into Meet, which means a leader runs the hour without leaving it and a learner never has to wonder which window matters.

You don't need a cohort to use it.

In any other Google Meet™ call, Human Shaped gives you teaching tools with no account at all. Press Start a session and you have slides with your own speaker notes, questions everyone answers with their name kept off the count, a way for everyone to propose ideas and vote on them, a clock, and reactions, all on the main stage. Nothing from the session is kept after it ends.

You can also show a website there, and everyone in the call gets their own working copy to use. Choose one of your own GitHub Pages sites or paste any link, or make a repository of your own to keep your slides and your site, change it during the call, and show the new version when you're ready. If you'd like to learn to build things like that from the beginning, our cohorts are free.

Adding it, and opening it in a call.

  1. Press Add it to Google Meet and install Human Shaped with the Google account you join calls with.
  2. Join your session from its calendar event or from the session's page on humanshaped.org.
  3. In the call, open Activities, choose Human Shaped, and sign in with Google or with GitHub.

When your leader opens the main stage, a notice appears at the top of Meet for a few seconds, and then a small icon stays in its place. Press it, then Join activity, and the main stage and your panel both open. If you have not installed the add-on yet, Meet offers it there too. When your leader launches breakouts, your panel gives you one button to your room: leave the main call, and open Human Shaped in the room. Your leader calls everyone back from the same place.

Meet runs add-ons only in a computer's browser, so join from a computer, though in an open room anyone can follow along on any device with the link the host copies from People. Some schools and workplaces turn add-ons off for their accounts, and if yours has, add it to a personal Google account and join the call with that one.

What it never does.

It never joins as a participant, so no bot or AI sits in the call with you, and it never gives anyone a score or compares people. What happens in the add-on is kept so that a session can be understood afterward: when you had it open, the reactions and stances you sent, and how the session moved. A leader can read this for everyone in their cohort, you can read what is kept about you on the session’s page, and no one else can. From Google it asks only to sign you in, which shares your name, email address, and profile picture, and the only thing it reads from Meet is the call's meeting code. A leader can record a session with Human Shaped's recorder, which opens in its own window, only after saying so out loud and in the chat, and everyone sees that it is recording. Software then works out from that recording who spoke, for how long, and what was said, and a leader can read it all while each person reads their own words. The privacy page says what is kept and for how long.
