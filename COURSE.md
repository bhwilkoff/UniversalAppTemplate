# Teaching with this template

I am building a class on values-based app development with AI around
this repository. I have not taught it yet. This page is the shape I
expect it to take, written down so that I can be wrong about it in
specific ways.

The course is the path in `docs/path/`. Each stage already ends with a
"Be ready to..." line, and that line is the week's work to bring back.
What follows is how the stages map onto a ten-week term, and what each
week asks a person to make.

## The one requirement

Every student builds one real app of their own choosing, for people
they can actually name. Not a tutorial app. Something they want to
exist. Every week's work is done on that app.

## Ten weeks

| Week | Stage | What students make | What they bring back |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00. Why we build | A "Why we build" paragraph, one standing rule about what AI will not do in their product, and the four questions run against their first idea. | The paragraph, the rule, and what the rule will cost them. |
| 2 | 01. The first prototype | The web app with twenty real records, in a list with all four states, running on their own phone. | The list on a phone, and one thing real data taught them. |
| 3 | 02. The shape of an app | Their data-plane decision, five verbs in `PARITY.md`, and one idiom looked up per platform. | The five rows, and the idiom they had to look up. |
| 4 | 03. Going native | A second platform, with a floor measured by test builds and recorded as a decision. | The oldest device the app runs on, and what it does not show there. |
| 5 | 03, continued | The first verb working natively on the second platform, with its parity row updated in the same commit. | A side-by-side of one verb in two idioms. |
| 6 | 04. Seeing it work | One launch door, a bench file, and one fix proven by a screenshot from a real device. | The screenshot, and what it would have shown if the fix had failed. |
| 7 | 05. Shipping | A build in a store's test track (TestFlight or Play internal testing), installed on a device they did not build it on. | The installed app, and the release notes they wrote. |
| 8 | 06. Keeping it running | Pulse configured for their app, reading at least one real source honestly. | One number Pulse read correctly, and one reader that said it could not. |
| 9 | 07. Raising the ceiling | One feature for their newest device, behind a capability check, with an honest parity row. | The feature, and the sentence an older device shows instead. |
| 10 | 08. Working with AI | One skill written from a lesson their app taught them, and their decision log in order. | The skill, and the mistake it will save the next person from. |

## What each class session is for

I expect each session to have three parts, in this order:

1. **Show what you brought back.** Each student shows their "Be ready
   to..." work to at least two others. The conversation is the point.
2. **Read one real decision together.** A short passage from
   `DECISIONS.md`, a skill, or one of the stories the stage opens with,
   and a discussion of what the builder wanted, what it cost, and
   whether we would choose the same.
3. **Build.** With the agent, on their own app, with the four questions
   on the wall.

## What I am unsure about

- **Ten weeks may be too few** for students who have never used a
  terminal. Week 1 might need a setup session before it.
- **Apple and Google developer accounts cost money** ($99 a year and $25
  once). Week 7 may need a path that ends at an installable build shared
  directly, for students who cannot or should not pay.
- **Real devices are unevenly distributed.** Some students own three,
  some own one. Stage 04 is written so that one phone is enough, and I
  want to test that promise.

If you teach with this before I do, I would love to hear what broke.
