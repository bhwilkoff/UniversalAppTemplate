Cohorts

Five weeks, with other people, for free.

A cohort is five weeks of building one real app of your own with an AI agent, beside a small group of people doing the same work. It is for adults who have never built an app and have a human-shaped problem in mind: something in their life that has gone unsolved for months or even years, that they cannot do themselves with the tools they have now, and that touches real people they can name (examples of each shape).

What each week asks is below, week by week, and open cohorts are listed at the bottom of this page.

What a week asks of you.

Each week you build in conversation with your agent, and you publish what you made. Once a week the cohort meets live, and you show that week's work to at least two other people, because the conversation is the point. What you explain is the decisions you made rather than the code the agent wrote, and nothing you answer is ever graded.

Every session is shaped by what the cohort needs that week, so no two are alike. Before you leave one, you send your agent the first prompt of the coming week, so the next round of writing, playing, and publishing has already begun.

What you need for Cohort Prep.

You need a GitHub account, your own copy of the template, an AI agent that can work in your repository, and a desktop computer. You bring your own AI, and it can be anything from a model running on your own computer to a Claude or Gemini subscription. The course shows the cheapest ways that work, and it never asks you to buy from one company.

You do not have to pay for an app store account to finish, either. Week four ends with your app on someone else's device, which a test track or a direct share can do for free.

You build beside a partner or two.

Inside a cohort you work in a group of three that stays together, so there is always someone to show your work to even when one person is away, and you always know who your partners are and what the partnership asks of you. Groups are easy to change when one is not working. Everyone in the cohort can see one another's repositories, commits, and progress, along with the upcoming sessions and the current week's work.

Your repository is the evidence.

Finishing a cohort earns a credential, and the evidence for it is the work itself. A web app of your own, live at a real address, is enough, and each further platform your app is published on adds a level. The credential’s own page says what each level asks for and how anyone can check one.

The principles are the agreement, and the rules are yours.

Everyone in a cohort builds by the Human-Shaped Principles, and they are what the cohort agrees on from the first day. Anything more formal than that belongs to the people actually doing the work together, so each cohort decides its own rules in its first session and changes them whenever it needs to.

Your work stays yours.

Your app lives in your own GitHub account. The hub reads your progress from GitHub rather than copying it, your classmates see your work only inside the cohort, and your app appears on the public apps page only if you choose to show it there. The privacy page lists everything the site keeps, who can see each part, and when it goes away.

Week by week

Every week is already written down.

Each week's work is a stage or two of the method in the Universal App Template. Each stage opens with something that really happened while an app was being built this way, and ends with a "Be ready to..." line, which is what you bring back and show. You can read every one before you join, or copy the template and start on your own.

Joining a cohort starts with GitHub.

No cohort is open to join right now, and when one opens, it will be listed here.

Joining uses your GitHub account, because that is where your app will live and where your cohort will talk. Choose a cohort below, sign in with GitHub, and you will be asked once more before you join.

If you do not have a GitHub account yet, it is free, and it is the first thing you will need either way.
