Cohorts

Five weeks, with other people, for free.

A cohort is about five weeks of building one real app of your own with an AI agent, with a live session each week and a small group of people doing the same work beside you. It is for adults who have never built an app and have a human-shaped problem in mind, one that touches real people they can name.

The first cohort's dates will be posted on this page, and joining uses your GitHub account.

A cohort, week by week

One live session a week, and something to show at the end of each one.

  1. BeforeYour app's own address, open on your phone
  2. Week 1Why it exists, and a first version running on real data
  3. Week 2The same app, on a second platform
  4. Week 3Proof that it works, and a look of its own
  5. Week 4Your app on someone else's device
  6. Week 5A feature you wanted, shown to everyone

One live session a week, and every one recorded.

Each week the cohort meets once on Google Meet. You show what you brought back to at least two other people, because the conversation is the point, and then we read one real prompt together and talk about what the builder wanted, what the agent got wrong, and how the prompt fixed it. After that, everyone builds, on their own app, with the four questions on the wall.

Every session has moments to stop and check what you understood, and what you explain back is the decisions you made rather than the code the agent wrote. Sessions are recorded and kept on Google Drive for everyone in the cohort, so missing a week never means falling behind.

What you need before the first week.

You need a GitHub account, your own copy of the template, an AI agent that can work in your repository, and a desktop computer. You bring your own AI, and it can be anything from a model running on your own computer to a Claude or Gemini subscription. The course shows the cheapest ways that work, and it never asks you to buy from one company.

You do not have to pay for an app store account to finish, either. Week four ends with your app on someone else's device, which a test track or a direct share can do for free.

You build beside a partner or two.

Inside a cohort you work in a pair, a trio or a small group, and you always know who your partners are and what the partnership asks of you. Groups are easy to change when one is not working. Everyone in the cohort can see one another's repositories, commits, and progress, along with the upcoming sessions and the current week's work.

Your repository is the evidence.

Finishing a cohort earns a credential, and the evidence for it is the work itself. A web app of your own, live at a real address, is enough, and each further platform your app is published on adds a level. People who hold the credential can return to finished cohorts and help newcomers as mentors.

Free, and anyone can teach one.

Cohorts are free, and so are the method and the tools. I am teaching the first cohorts myself, then people I invite, and in time anyone who wants to lead a cohort of their own will be able to, which is how a method like this reaches more people than one teacher ever could.

How we treat one another.

There is no code of conduct to sign, because something this light should not start with something that heavy. How we treat one another rests on the Human-Shaped Principles and on my Educational Model Spec, and we say it plainly where people actually meet, at the start of every cohort.

What we keep, and what we never own.

Your work lives in your own GitHub account, and it stays yours. The hub reads your progress from GitHub with your permission rather than copying it, keeps only your membership and what you choose to share while you are in a cohort, and lets go of all of it when you leave. It never owns your software, and what it does keep is stored in the United States.

Cohort conversations happen in the humanshaped organization on GitHub, and they are private to the cohort unless the cohort decides to open them.

The path is open before you join.

Everything a cohort follows is already written down, so you can read the whole path today, or copy the template and start on your own.
