# Allies, round 1: organizations, campaigns, and civic movements for human-first AI

Researched 2026-10-08. **Checked** means a page was opened or a search result read in this session on 2026-10-08 (the source is named). **From memory** means it comes from prior knowledge and was not confirmed today. Search summaries from aggregators (longtermwiki, grantedai, opportunity boards) are marked as secondary.

Principle numbers used below: 1 human-shaped problem; 2 capability over profit; 3 written values as guardrails; 4 iteration; 5 research; 6 decisions with feedback; 7 tested as people use it; 8 documentation; 9 share openly; 10 learning counts; 11 people own their data; 12 no new human problems; 13 own voice; 14 credit everyone, AI included; 15 joy.

A tension that runs through the whole list: most of these groups criticize the frontier labs whose models (Claude, Gemini) Human Shaped teaches people to build with. Several (DAIR, AI Now, Fairplay, the Luddite revival) would see "learn to build with Claude" as part of the problem. The honest answer Human Shaped can give is its own: AI builds the tool, the tool limits what AI does to people, the work is in public, and the agent is credited. Expect to be asked about it.

## 1. Humanity AI (philanthropic coalition, open call closing soon)

- **URL:** https://humanityai.ai/ (note: humanityai.org is a parked domain). Open call: https://humanityai.ai/open-call
- **What it does:** A $500 million, five-year coalition of funders (Ford, MacArthur, Omidyar Network, Mellon, Mozilla, Packard, Lumina, Kapor, Doris Duke, Siegel Family Endowment) "to build a more human(e) future in which AI is shaped by and for people." Its levers are widening who makes AI decisions, strengthening organizations that bring people together, and public goods that put AI in people's hands. **Checked** (humanityai.ai, 2026-10-08; member names partly from logos and from secondary search results).
- **Open call "Communities Leading on AI":** $10 million, grants $75,000 to $1,000,000 for up to three years; initial applications due **October 21, 2026, 4:59 p.m. PT**; finalists notified December 2026. Lead applicant must be a US 501(c)(3), a fiscal sponsor for an individual or informal group, a tribal government, or a public university; projects must serve US-based communities. Issue areas: education; labor and economy; humanities, arts, and culture. Approaches: shaping how AI is governed and used, **building AI on community terms**, challenging AI that does not serve communities. Open-weight or open-source models are prioritized where models are central. **Out of scope: AI literacy, AI adoption, and research-only work.** **Checked** (humanityai.ai/open-call, 2026-10-08).
- **Shares:** 1, 2, 9, 11, 12. **Differs:** the open-model priority and the "AI literacy is out of scope" line both cut against a course that teaches people to build with Claude or Gemini. A pitch would have to be about communities building their own tools on their own terms (the apps and the teacher network), not about teaching AI skills.
- **How to join in:** Decide this week whether a proposal is realistic (needs a 501(c)(3) lead or fiscal sponsor; check whether Learning is Change, Inc. qualifies). A collaboration application with a partner (for example a library, a union local, or a Data & Society AI Civics anchor partner) is allowed and prioritized when led by a non-2026 grantee. Join the mailing list regardless; the coalition says pooled funding continues.
- **Inaugural grantees (May 2026):** secondary sources say about $8 million to groups including AI Now, Data & Society, DAIR, and others, with conflicting lists. **Checked, secondary only** (search results 2026-10-08; not confirmed on humanityai.ai).
- **People:** John Palfrey (MacArthur president) is quoted in coverage. **Checked, secondary.**

## 3. Collective Intelligence Project (CIP)

- **URL:** https://www.cip.org/
- **What it does:** Builds tools for democratic input into AI. Current projects: **Weval** (an open platform where anyone builds qualitative evaluations of models for what matters to them), **Global Dialogues** (large structured deliberations producing an open dataset on how people experience AI), **Community Models** (communities write a "constitution" and refine a model to it), Jhilmil (regional cultural evaluations), and Alignment Assemblies. **Checked** (cip.org, 2026-10-08).
- **Shares:** 3 (written values as guardrails is close to community constitutions), 6, 7, 9, 11. **Differs:** CIP works directly with frontier labs, so it will not object to Claude or Gemini; its focus is governing models, not teaching people to build apps.
- **How to join in:** Most concrete door on this list for a small community: a cohort could build a **Weval evaluation** of how well coding agents follow a human-shaped method (for example, does the agent ask the method's questions, does it label itself). Contribute questions to Global Dialogues (CIP invites outside groups to submit questions). Ask about a Community Model built from PRINCIPLES.md. **Checked** (cip.org lists Weval, Global Dialogues Challenge, partnering; question submission from search summary).
- **People:** Divya Siddarth (co-founder, executive director), Saffron Huang (co-founder, board), Evan Hadfield (projects lead). **Checked** (cip.org/team, 2026-10-08).

## 5. Pro-Human AI Declaration (Future of Life Institute coalition)

- **URL:** https://humanstatement.org/
- **What it does:** A March 2026 statement that "artificial intelligence should serve humanity, not the reverse," with 33 principles in five sections: keeping humans in charge; avoiding concentration of power; protecting the human experience (including bot-or-not labeling and no deceptive AI identity); human agency and liberty (data rights, avoiding enfeeblement of users); and accountability for AI companies. Signatories listed include Yoshua Bengio, Stuart Russell, Randi Weingarten, Mustafa Suleyman, Steve Bannon, Susan Rice, and 313 organizations including the AFL-CIO Tech Institute, SAG-AFTRA, FLI, and the Center for Humane Technology. **Checked** (humanstatement.org, 2026-10-08). Organized from a January 2026 meeting in New Orleans convened by Max Tegmark; a Washington "Pro-Human Assembly" followed. **Checked, secondary** (news summaries).
- **Shares:** 11, 12, 13 (no deceptive AI identity), and the "avoid enfeeblement" idea matches 10. **Differs:** FLI's center of gravity is catastrophic-risk and superintelligence policy; the coalition is deliberately cross-partisan and includes figures some Human Shaped members will not want to stand beside. Pure policy statement, no building.
- **How to join in:** Organizations and individuals can endorse through a form (institutional email preferred, verified by a person). Low cost, but signing ties Human Shaped's name to that whole roster; a decision for Ben, not a default.
- **People:** Max Tegmark (FLI). **Checked, secondary.**

## 16. Not By AI and the Authors Guild "Human Authored" mark (human-made labels)

- **URLs:** https://notbyai.fyi/ ; https://authorsguild.org/news/human-authored-certification-expands-to-all-authors/
- **Not By AI:** Badges for work that is at least 90% human-made, self-assessed ("not an AI detection tool"); AI allowed for inspiration, grammar checks, legal boilerplate, and non-user-facing text such as SEO tags. Free for non-commercial projects; commercial from $5 a month or $99 once. Its "Written by Human" badge explicitly covers **code**. **Checked** (notbyai.fyi, 2026-10-08). Founder Allen Hsu, launched March 2023. **Checked, secondary.**
- **Human Authored:** Opened to all US-published authors March 2, 2026 ($10 a title for non-members, free for members, identity verification, a license, ten titles a year, public lookup database). Text must be written by humans; AI may be used for research, brainstorming, outlining, indexes. Books only. **Checked** (authorsguild.org, 2026-10-08). Over 3,000 authors and 5,000 titles certified. **Checked, secondary.**
- **Shares:** 13 directly, 14. **Differs:** both labels define "human-made" by the absence of AI in the output. Human Shaped's apps are written with AI agents, so "Not By AI" for code would be false for them. The honest fit is narrower: Human Shaped's *words* (site copy, a student's README in their own voice) could carry a "Written by Human" badge; the code cannot.
- **How to join in:** Use both as reference points when designing Human Shaped's own mark (the brand kit's "declare" marks), and say plainly how it differs: a declaration of how and for whom something was built, crediting the AI, rather than a claim of no AI.
- **People:** Mary Rasenberger (Authors Guild CEO). **Checked, secondary.**

## 21. Policy frames to align with and cite

- **UNESCO AI competency frameworks** (launched Digital Learning Week, September 2024): student framework with four areas (human-centred mindset, ethics of AI, AI techniques and applications, AI system design; 12 competencies, levels Understand, Apply, Create); teacher framework with five areas (adds AI pedagogy and AI for professional learning; 15 competencies). UNESCO says AI should support teachers, not take over their roles, and warns against using AI to solve teacher shortages. **Checked** (unesco.org, 2026-10-08; competency counts secondary). Human Shaped's weeks map well to "AI system design" at the "Create" level. Fengchun Miao is lead author.
- **OECD and European Commission AI Literacy Framework (AILit)**, final version presented June 17 or 18, 2026: four domains (engage with AI, create with AI, manage AI, shape AI), reported 19 competences, basic to advanced; developed with support from Code.org (now reported as "CodeAI"); feeds the PISA 2029 Media and AI Literacy assessment. **Checked, secondary** (search results including an EC representation page). "Create with AI" and "shape AI" are the domains to cite.
- **EU AI Act Article 4 (AI literacy):** applied from February 2, 2025; the Digital Omnibus (reported as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force July 27, 2026) softened it to "take measures to support" AI literacy, without requiring any set level. **Checked, secondary** (compliance blogs; not confirmed on EUR-Lex). Matters only if a cohort serves EU organizations; a free cohort is a way staff can meet the duty.
- **Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights** (OSTP, October 2022): five principles (safe and effective systems, protection from algorithmic discrimination, data privacy, notice and explanation, human alternatives and fallback). Non-binding; Executive Order 14110 was revoked January 20, 2025 and the Blueprint was removed from whitehouse.gov. **From memory** for the five principles and the removal; non-binding status **Checked, secondary**. Cite as a historical frame, not current US policy. The March 20, 2026 White House National Policy Framework for AI favors preempting state laws (secondary).
- **OECD AI Principles** (2019, updated May 2024). **From memory.**

## Strongest five to approach first, and why

1. **Collective Intelligence Project.** It already works with the labs Human Shaped uses, so there is no stance conflict, and Weval gives a cohort a concrete, publishable project: an open evaluation of whether coding agents follow a human-shaped method. That is principles 3, 6, 9 in practice, and it produces something CIP can use.
2. **Humanity AI open call, as a collaboration.** $75,000 to $1,000,000, deadline October 21, 2026. Only worth it if Human Shaped pitches communities building their own tools (not AI literacy, which is out of scope), names a 501(c)(3) lead or fiscal sponsor, and answers the open-model priority honestly (see Public AI). Even if this round is too soon, it shows what the field's main funder wants.
3. **Data & Society AI Civics.** A brand-new, two-year, train-the-trainer program building a national civic coalition, led by Meg Young. Human Shaped's many-teachers model fits, and it could also be the collaboration partner for item 2.
4. **Design Justice Network.** Small, principle-led, signable today, active in 2026, and its principles are the closest outside statement of "start from a human-shaped problem" and "tested the way people actually use it." Signing and citing costs nothing and is easy to explain.
5. **Public AI Network.** Not for money but for integrity: it is the best answer to the critique most of this list will raise. A cohort experiment using a free public model (Apertus or SEA-LION through the Inference Utility) for the AI reviewer or inside a student app would let Human Shaped say it tried the public option, in public.

Close runners-up: All Tech Is Human (cheapest reach to many people), the NEA and AFT (teachers as cohort students and teachers), and the Pro-Human AI Declaration (easy to sign, but the roster is a judgment call for Ben).
