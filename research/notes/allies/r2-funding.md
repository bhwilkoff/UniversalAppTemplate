## 3. Open calls through mid-2027

### Humanity AI "Communities Leading on AI" open call. **Checked** (humanityai.ai/open-call, 2026-10-08)
- **Deadline confirmed: 4:59 p.m. PT / 7:59 p.m. ET, Wednesday, October 21, 2026** (13 days from today).
- **Eligibility:** US-based organizations, collaborations, and individuals. "Lead organizations must have 501(c)(3) status." Individuals and informal groups apply through a US 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor that "must be responsible for overseeing and administering the grant," or by joining a collaboration whose lead is a 501(c)(3) or fiscal sponsor. For-profits may join collaborations but may not lead. Learning is Change, Inc. cannot lead.
- **Tiers:** $75K to $150K; $150K to $500K; $500K to $1M.
- **Issue areas:** education ("particularly interested in work led by students, educators, and the organizations who represent them"); labor and economy; humanities, arts, and culture.
- **Approaches:** shaping how AI is governed and used; **"Building AI on community terms": technical efforts that center community needs over profit, with priority on AI infrastructure**; challenging AI that does not serve communities.
- **Out of scope, verbatim:** "AI Literacy: Efforts that are primarily about providing the knowledge and skills to use AI tools"; "AI Adoption: Efforts that are primarily about promoting the spread of specific AI tools and systems"; "Research-only."
- **Open models:** "Where AI models are part of the proposed work's core methodology or deliverables, priority will be given to open-weight and/or open-source models over closed models."
- **Could "communities building their own tools with AI under written values" fit?** Possibly, under education plus "building AI on community terms," but only if the proposal is genuinely about educators and students deciding what software their communities need and owning it, with the principles as the community's written terms. The weak points are real: the word "primarily" in the exclusions means a reviewer reading "a course that teaches people to build with Claude or Gemini" will see AI literacy and adoption of specific tools; the community-terms approach prioritizes AI infrastructure, which Human Shaped does not build; and the open-model priority cuts against a method run on Claude and Gemini. A credible pitch would need a named 501(c)(3) lead and would have to say plainly how it would test open models (see the Public AI note in r1-ethics.md). My read: **a long shot in 13 days**, worth it only as a collaboration with an existing 501(c)(3) education partner that would lead.

### MIT Solve 2027 Global Learning Challenge. **Checked** (solve.mit.edu/challenges/2027-global-learning-challenge)

- **Stage:** Prototype, Pilot, Growth, or Scale; concept-stage ideas only in the Indigenous Communities Fellowship. Human Shaped has a working template, site, and hub, which reads as a prototype; a first cohort would make it a pilot.

- **Timeline:** finalists notified in February, virtual pitch, then selection.

## 4. Candid assessment

**First, the precondition.** Human Shaped has no 501(c)(3) of its own, so nothing on this list that requires one can be led by it.

**Worth Ben's time now:**

1. **MIT Solve Global Learning Challenge (November 2).** No 501(c)(3) requirement found, the bar is "prototype," the question ("close global education gaps while improving outcomes for every learner") matches a free course that anyone may teach, and the AI for Humanity Prize is the only realistic door to McGovern. **Independence cost: low.** The money is a grant, the commitment is about 25 hours plus two trips over nine months, and Solve does not take equity. The risk is mostly time, and the travel cost if not covered (unchecked).
2. **A fiscal sponsor, chosen slowly (Social Good Fund or CNDC).** Not a grant, but the key that unlocks every 501(c)(3)-only door (Humanity AI next time, Claude for Nonprofits, GitHub for Nonprofits, most foundations). **Independence cost: moderate and mostly financial.** A sponsor holds the money and has legal control over it (under Model A the project is legally the sponsor's program), takes 8 to 10%, and must approve grant applications; Social Good Fund also charges $29 a month until $5,000 comes in. At $0 raised, CNDC costs nothing. Neither tells Ben what to teach.
3. **Humanity AI (October 21), only as a collaboration.** Worth one email this week to a 501(c)(3) education partner (a teachers' union local, a library, a Denver-area nonprofit, or Data & Society's AI Civics) asking whether they would lead. Do not go alone through a brand-new sponsor in 13 days. **Independence cost: high if funded.** A $75K+ grant brings reporting, a lead partner that controls the money, and pressure to reshape the method toward open models and away from naming Claude or Gemini. That last pressure may be healthy (it is the Public AI critique in r1-ethics.md), but it should be Ben's choice, not a grant's.

**Skip for now:** NLnet (European focus, says it is not interested in AI-generated projects), Prototype Fund (Germany only), NumFOCUS (closed), Conservancy (months, projects under a year old ineligible), Players Philanthropy Fund ($25K minimum), HCB (teens only), Claude Corps hosting (needs an office and supervisor), Spencer (research and invitation only), Knight and Siegel (no open doors).

**On free credits:** they do keep students at $0, and Human Shaped can tell students about them (Copilot Student, the US college Gemini offer through December 31, Claude for Teachers if confirmed), but the course should never require one, should name both Claude and Gemini side by side, and should warn about auto-renewal. Taking company money or company-branded programs for the course itself is where independence actually gets spent; telling students about free offers that already exist costs nothing.

### Draft project description (for Ben to rewrite in his own voice)

> **DRAFT, not Ben's words.** Human Shaped is a free, five-week online course in which people who are not professional programmers build software for a problem in their own lives or communities, working with an AI coding agent and a teacher. Each person starts from a human problem, writes down the values their software must keep, tests it with the people it is for, and publishes it openly; the AI agent asks the method's questions and is always labeled as AI, and it never replaces a teacher's feedback. Anyone may become a teacher and lead a cohort, the materials are openly licensed, and the whole thing runs at no cost to students or to us. Students own their work: it lives in their own repositories, and our hub only shows what they choose to share. We want to grow the number of teachers, open cohorts to educators, workers, and community groups, and test whether open AI models can do the same work, so that communities decide what software they need instead of waiting for someone to sell it to them.
