## 6. AI contribution policies, and the crediting convention

- **Rust: Corrected.** Not "banned by default." On **August 5, 2026**,
  five teams adopted an LLM policy for the **rust-lang/rust monorepo
  only**; the post says it is "not an official stance on LLMs" for the
  whole project. The rule: "It's fine to use LLMs to answer questions,
  analyze, distill, refine, check, suggest, review. But not to
  **create**." LLM-originated changes are allowed only when pre-arranged,
  non-critical, tested, and disclosed; "You must disclose LLM-generated
  content"; "You may not hide LLM involvement"; LLM review cannot replace
  human review. Disclosure is through a coming PR template question, **no
  commit trailer**. Author: Jynn Nelson. **Checked** (Inside Rust blog,
  2026-08-05). A secondary claim of a "circuit breaker" at half of merges
  is not in the post; do not repeat it.
- **Fedora: Confirmed.** The Council approved its AI-Assisted
  Contributions Policy on **October 22, 2025** (v1.0, 2025-10-24, credited
  to Jason Brooks and the Council). "You MAY use AI assistance";
  "The contributor is always the author and is fully accountable"; "You
  MUST disclose the use of AI tools when the significant part of the
  contribution is taken from a tool without changes. You SHOULD disclose
  the other uses"; AI must not be "the sole or final arbiter" of
  judgments on contributions or people. **Checked** (policy text via
  search snippets of docs.fedoraproject.org mirrors; Phoronix and LWN,
  October 2025). That it names an `Assisted-by` trailer comes from
  Phoronix; I could not see the trailer in the policy text (the docs
  site blocks the fetcher). **Partly unknown.**
- **Linux kernel: Confirmed.** docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants:
  "Assisted-by: LLM [TOOL1] [TOOL2]" where the tools are specialized
  analysis tools (coccinelle, sparse, smatch, clang-tidy), not git or
  editors; "AI agents MUST NOT add Signed-off-by tags"; "Only humans can
  legally certify the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO)." **Checked.**
  The September 2026 study says the kernel **dropped** an earlier
  requirement to name the model, to "avoid free advertising to
  proprietary software companies," which matches the generic "LLM" in
  today's text. **Checked.**
- **OSI Open Source AI Definition: Confirmed, unchanged.** Version 1.0
  (released October 2024) is still "the first stable version"; no 2025
  or 2026 revision on opensource.org/ai. It defines open AI systems, not
  how to credit AI in code. **Checked.**
- **Software Freedom Conservancy: Confirmed.** "LLM-backed generative AI
  recommendations" (sfconservancy.org/llm-gen-ai/; LWN covered it June
  2026, secondary date). Best practices, not requirements; supports
  maintainers who refuse AI contributions; recommendation 5 calls full
  disclosure "a moral imperative": name the system, its version, and how
  it helped, "in a machine-readable format in commit logs." **No trailer
  key named.** **Checked.**
- **The study: Confirmed.** Hora, Robbes, Zacchiroli, "'We Permit the Use
  of AI, but [...]': The Landscape of AI Policies in Popular Open Source
  Projects," arXiv 2609.07542, September 7, 2026. Of 281 policies: 76.2%
  permit AI in code and 7.1% encourage it (83.3%); 14.9% forbid it; 48.8%
  require disclosure (Table 1 says 53.4%, an inconsistency in the paper);
  disclosure goes in the PR description (64.7%) more than the commit
  message (26.7%); about half of dedicated policy files have been revised.
  Trailer examples: nextcloud requires "Assisted-by:" on each AI-assisted
  commit; RustPython uses "Assisted-by: Claude Code:claude-sonnet-4-6" and
  says not to use Co-authored-by; psf/requests says "remove any LLM
  co-author tags and take full ownership of every line"; bitcoin's
  commit says "discourage adding AI agents as commit authors"; but
  apache/kafka asks for "Co-Authored-By, Generated-by, or similar."
  **Checked** (arXiv HTML).
- **The convention emerging (my synthesis of the above):** disclose, and
  keep a human as the author and the one accountable. Where a trailer is
  named, it is most often **`Assisted-by:`**, with the human's own
  sign-off; several projects explicitly reject `Co-authored-by` for
  agents because it makes the tool an author; a minority accept it. No
  single standard exists, and the biggest projects (Rust, SFC) ask for
  disclosure without fixing a key. For Human Shaped, which uses
  `Co-Authored-By` today, this strengthens round 1's suggestion of a
  decision entry: `Assisted-by: <agent>` plus the student as sole author
  would match the kernel, Fedora, nextcloud, and RustPython.

## 7. Learner AI policies

- **freeCodeCamp: Confirmed.** The Academic Honesty Policy pledge now
  reads: "I pledge that I did not use AI tools to generate my solution
  unless the lesson explicitly said it was OK to do so," and "Even though
  AI tools are useful, they are no substitute for actually learning
  concepts yourself." It keeps "100% of the code in my projects was
  written by me" (or pair-programmed live). Certifications are revoked
  for plagiarism. **Checked** (freecodecamp.org/news/academic-honesty-policy/;
  the post carries a 2019 date but the AI lines are newer).
- **The Odin Project: Confirmed.** Foundations lesson "Motivation and
  mindset," section "A note on AI code generation," ends: "We do not
  recommend using AI tools for your learning." Reasons given: missing how
  things work, not learning to ask people (its Discord), not learning to
  explain your code, new learners cannot judge output, and interviews
  often bar AI. Cites David Humphrey's "CheatGPT." **Checked** (GitHub,
  TheOdinProject/curriculum, read through the GitHub API).
- **Hack Club: Corrected (no single org-wide policy found; rules are per
  program).** Examples, all **Checked** in hackclub repositories on
  GitHub: Horizons FAQ, "Use of generative AI **for graphics and audio**
  is **prohibited**. Use of generative AI for other purposes is allowed,
  but we will not accept projects which qualify as 'AI slop'," and no AI
  for updates or descriptions; Stardance AI_POLICY.md, "All AI usage in
  any form must be disclosed," you must be able to explain your change
  "without an AI tool holding your hand," no AI-generated media; Sprig
  gallery, "Substantially AI-generated games may not be approved"; Forge
  and Highway, journals and write-ups in your own words, AI-generated text
  rejected. Summer of Making's review code flags README content as
  `AI_GENERATED`. The pattern: AI allowed for code with disclosure and
  understanding, banned for art and for the teen's own writing.
- **What this means for Human Shaped:** all three are learn-to-code
  programs that keep AI away from the learner's core work; Human Shaped
  teaches building *with* agents. Hack Club's "disclose, understand it,
  write your own words" is the closest match to principles 13 and 14.
