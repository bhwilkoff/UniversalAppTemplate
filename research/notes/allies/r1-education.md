# Allies, round 1: educational organizations and learning movements

Researched 2026-10-08 for humanshaped.org. Area: peer-learning and cohort communities, learning-by-building movements, and groups working on human-first, learner-owned use of AI.

How facts are marked:

- **Checked 2026-10-08**: I opened the page this session.
- **Search only**: the fact came from search-result summaries this session, and I did not open the source page. Treat it as a lead to confirm.
- **From memory**: my own prior knowledge, not checked this session.

Principle numbers follow the site's list: 1 human-shaped problem; 2 increase what people can do, not profit; 3 values written down as guardrails; 4 iteration; 5 grounded in research, knowing whose work you build on; 6 decisions with feedback; 7 tested the way people use it; 8 documentation as seriously as code; 9 share openly; 10 learning counts as success; 11 people own their data; 12 create no new human problems; 13 the builder's own voice; 14 credit everyone, including AI agents; 15 bring joy to at least one person.

P2PU, the Carpentries, Coderetreat, Code for America brigades, and Mozilla clubs are already in the toolkit research, so P2PU appears below only for what is new.

## 1. Recurse Center

- **URL:** https://www.recurse.com/ ; AI position: https://www.recurse.com/blog/191-developing-our-position-on-ai
- **What they do:** A free, self-directed "retreat for curious programmers": batches of about 30 people for 6 or 12 weeks, in Brooklyn or remote, with new batches every six weeks. It charges no tuition and is funded by a recruiting agency that partner companies pay when they hire. Its homepage now lists "Figuring out how to code with agents?" as an example of what someone might work on. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)
- **AI stance:** "Developing our position on AI" (Nick Bergson-Shilcock with Emily Bernier and Rachel Petacat, July 23, 2025) takes a nondogmatic position. It ties AI use to RC's three self-directives and says "You should use AI-powered tools to complement or increase your agency, not replace it." It warns that LLMs can widen the gap between what you can produce and what you understand, and that they can draw people away from asking each other questions. It deliberately takes no stance on wider societal questions such as energy or jobs. An April 24, 2026 post on redesigning the application covers spotting LLM-written applications. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)
- **Shared principles:** 2, 4, 9, 10 (learning is the point, "learn generously"), 13 (concern about generated applications), 15. The agency sentence is very close to Human Shaped's stance on the student's own agent.
- **Where they differ:** RC is unstructured on purpose, with no curriculum, no teacher, and no "problem first" requirement, and it is a recruiting business. It takes no position on whether software should serve people over profit.
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Cite the July 2025 post on /about/ or in the teaching guide as a peer stance on AI and agency. Invite a Recurser (RC lists alumni on its site) to give a guest talk in a cohort. Point graduates who want an unstructured next step toward RC, which is free like the cohorts. RC runs public events in its space (**From memory**), which could host a Human Shaped meetup.
- **Key people:** Nick Bergson-Shilcock (cofounder, **Checked** as author); David Albert and Sonali Sridhar (cofounders, **From memory**).

## 2. Hack Club

- **URL:** https://hackclub.com/ ; team: https://hackclub.com/team/ ; Macondo FAQ: https://macondo.hackclub.com/docs/faq
- **What they do:** A 501(c)(3), free for teens aged 13 to 18, with 1,500+ high school clubs, a large Slack, and "You Ship, We Ship" style programs where teens build projects and ship them for rewards. Recent events on the homepage include Daydream (100 game jams in 100 cities on one weekend), Undercity (a hardware hackathon at GitHub HQ), and Stardance. HCB gives 700+ teen-led teams fiscal sponsorship. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)
- **AI stance:** Macondo (2026, hours count from April 15, ends late August) says "Projects that are fully vibe coded or generated by AI will be rejected," and expects teens to build most of the project themselves, with AI helping on ideas or editing. (**Checked 2026-10-08**) The homepage states no general AI policy.
- **Shared principles:** 4, 9 (everything ships in public), 10, 15, and a strong "make things you care about" culture close to 1.
- **Where they differ:** Teens only, so no overlap in students, but strong overlap in method. Its AI rule is stricter than Human Shaped's, which treats the agent as the builder's main tool, so Hack Club would likely see a Human Shaped app as "generated by AI" unless the student's own writing, decisions, and play are visible. Prize-for-shipping incentives sit uneasily with "learning counts as success."
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Offer a Human Shaped workshop or a "write, play, publish" session for club leaders, adapted to Hack Club's AI rule (the student writes the problem and values, the agent helps). Hack Club alumni turning 19 are a natural audience for the adult cohorts. HCB's fiscal-sponsorship model is worth studying if teachers ever need to take donations for local events.
- **Key people:** Zach Latta (founder and Executive Director), Christina Asquith (cofounder and President), Max Wofford (Tech and Creative Lead). (**Checked 2026-10-08**)

## 3. School for Poetic Computation (SFPC)

- **URL:** https://sfpc.study/ ; https://sfpc.study/about ; https://sfpc.study/participate
- **What they do:** "An experimental school in New York City and online" for art, code, hardware, and critical theory. Fall 2026 runs ten online classes, September 21 to December 3, in 5- or 10-week formats with two time options each (for example V Buckenham's "Tools for Creativity," 5 weeks, and "The Digital Will: Making, Sorting, and Memorializing Our Data"). Winter 2027 classes are announced in late October. Tuition is paid, with subsidized seats in every class on an honor-system, pay-what-you-can basis. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)
- **AI stance:** None stated on the pages I opened. Its framing ("decolonization and transformative justice") suggests critical engagement. (**Checked 2026-10-08** for the absence)
- **Shared principles:** 1, 5, 9, 11 (classes on data and memorial), 12, 13, 15. The 5-week class format is the same length as a Human Shaped cohort.
- **Where they differ:** Art and critique first, not app building. Paid tuition. SFPC "does not accept unsolicited class proposals," so a Human Shaped teacher cannot simply propose a class.
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Attend events and follow the newsletter, which is how SFPC says teaching opportunities are announced. Link to SFPC from the teaching guide as a model for small, 5-week, teacher-shaped classes. Its online showcase and events are a place to show Human Shaped apps that are also personal or poetic.
- **Key people:** Co-Directors Todd Anderson and Neta Bomani; founders Taeyoon Choi, Zach Lieberman, Jen Lowe, and Amit Pitaru. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)

- **Where they differ:** Creative coding, not human problems. Its AI thinking centers on stewardship of open infrastructure and ethics, and the Coding Train page states no position on generative AI.
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Host a Processing Community Day event (community-led, anyone can organize one, **From memory** on the process) where people build a small human-shaped tool in p5.js with an agent. Watch for the next fellowship open call. Submit cohort work that uses p5.js to the Coding Train Passenger Showcase.
- **Key people:** Roxana Hadad and Xin Xin (Co-Executive Directors), Raphaël de Courville (Processing Community Lead) (**Checked 2026-10-08**); Daniel Shiffman (**Checked** as Coding Train host).

## 5. Raspberry Pi Foundation (Code Club, Experience AI)

- **URL:** https://www.raspberrypi.org/ ; position paper post: https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/why-kids-still-need-to-learn-to-code-in-the-age-of-ai/ ; LLM unit post: https://www.raspberrypi.org/blog/encouraging-learners-to-think-first-prompt-second-using-large-language-models-to-learn/
- **What they do:** Runs Code Club (a global network of coding clubs, **Checked**) and Experience AI (lessons on how AI works, with Google DeepMind). On June 4, 2025, CEO Philip Colligan published "Why kids still need to learn to code in the age of AI," with five reasons, including that coding is "a literacy that supports agency" and that access shapes who builds the future. On September 28, 2026, Rehana Al-Soltane described a new five-lesson unit, "Using LLMs strategically for learning," for ages 13 to 16, which teaches "feedback literacy" (telling, guiding, challenging) and prompting for learning rather than answers. (**Checked 2026-10-08**)
- **Shared principles:** 5, 10, 13, and the unit's "think first, prompt second" is close to Human Shaped's review skill, which asks questions instead of giving answers.
- **Where they differ:** Children and schools, not adults building real apps. Corporate partnership with Google DeepMind. The position paper argues coding by hand still matters, which a Human Shaped course built around agents should answer honestly rather than ignore.
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Cite the telling/guiding/challenging framework in the course's review skill and teaching guide, with credit. Code Club is volunteer-run (**From memory**: volunteers start clubs in schools and libraries), so Human Shaped teachers could start an adult or family Code Club. Pitch a guest post to the Foundation's Hello World magazine for educators (**From memory**: it takes educator submissions).
- **Key people:** Philip Colligan (CEO), Rehana Al-Soltane (**Checked 2026-10-08**); Sue Sentance (research, **From memory**).

## 6. MIT Lifelong Kindergarten and the Scratch Foundation

(**Search only**; his Medium returned 403)

- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Cite Resnick's 2023 and 2025 essays alongside constructionism on /about/. Watch Scratch Foundation events for educator sessions on AI. Resnick spoke on "Agency and Creative Learning in the AI Era" at the Smilegate Future Lab conference in June 2026 (**Search only**), a sign he is open to this conversation.

## 10. Civics of Technology

- **URL:** https://www.civicsoftechnology.org/ ; https://www.civicsoftechnology.org/2026conference
- **What they do:** Research, curriculum, and professional development that help teachers and students examine how technology affects individual and collective life, starting from the view that technologies are not neutral. (**Checked 2026-10-08**) The 5th annual online conference, "Against Tech Hype" (August 6 and 7, 2026, free, keynotes by Meredith Broussard and Natasha Singer), questioned the push for rapid AI adoption in schools. (**Checked 2026-10-08**) The 2025 conference theme was "Communal Resistance to Artificial Systems." (**Search only**)
- **Shared principles:** 5, 11, 12, and the critical stance behind 2.
- **Where they differ:** This is the honest counterweight. Many in this community favor refusing or resisting generative AI in schools, and would challenge a course built on using agents at all.
- **How Human Shaped could join in:** Propose a session for the 2027 conference that faces the disagreement directly ("What would it take for AI-assisted software to create no new human problems?"). They seek partners to pilot curriculum, through their contact page. Read their curriculum when writing principle 12, and credit it. Being welcomed here would be strong evidence that "human-shaped" means something.
- **Key people:** Dan Krutka, Marie Heath, Jacob Pleasants (conference organizers, **Checked 2026-10-08**).

## Strongest five to approach first, and why

1. **Recurse Center.** Free, peer-run, adult, and it has already published the most thoughtful AI stance in this list, almost the same as Human Shaped's ("complement or increase your agency, not replace it"). Citing it costs nothing, and its alumni are exactly the people who could teach cohorts.
2. **Civics of Technology.** The strongest honest critic. A session at their free online conference, or a curriculum conversation, would test principle 12 and the movement's credibility with skeptical teachers; if Human Shaped can be welcomed there, it can be welcomed anywhere.
3. **Raspberry Pi Foundation (Code Club and the new LLM unit).** Its September 2026 "think first, prompt second" unit and its telling/guiding/challenging feedback framework line up with Human Shaped's review skill, and Code Club gives volunteers a ready way to run local sessions.
4. **P2PU and libraries running AI learning circles (TPL, EIFL's network).** Libraries already gather adults to learn about AI with a facilitator rather than an expert. A library-ready version of Cohort Prep is the most direct route to "anyone can teach." EIFL's October 26 deadline and TPL's grant ending in December 2026 make the timing good.
5. **Hack Club.** The largest building-and-shipping movement there is, with a clear AI rule that Human Shaped can respect. Its members aging out at 19 are a natural next audience, and its event formats (Daydream's 100 cities on one weekend) are a model for Human Shaped events.

Close behind: **Fab Academy** (the best working model of many local teachers sharing one course), **AI Pedagogy Project** (an easy, credited assignment contribution), and **CSTA 2027** (confirm the proposal deadline before October 21).
