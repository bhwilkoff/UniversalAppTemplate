# Allies, round 2: the gaps round 1 left

Researched October 8, 2026. Round 1 was mostly US and English-speaking, so
this round covers six gaps: groups outside the US, disability-led technology,
Indigenous data sovereignty, teacher networks, older adults with faith and
youth groups, and precedents for a "this software is human-shaped" mark.

**Checked** means I confirmed it this session on the group's own site or in a
search result that quoted it. **From memory** means I did not confirm it today.
Anything already in round 1 is only referenced here: Te Hiku Media, CARE,
Abundant Intelligences, the Indigenous Protocol and AI Working Group, Mila's
Indigenous Pathfinders, CSTA as an organization, Not By AI, the Authors Guild's
Human Authored mark, Design Justice Network, and AFT and NEA.

Numbers in the text, such as "principle 9", refer to PRINCIPLES.md v0.6.

## 1. Outside the US

### Europe

**1.1 Prototype Fund (Germany).** https://prototypefund.de/en
- *What it does:* It gives public money to individuals and small teams who
  build open-source, public-interest software. The current round is "class
  03", and its application window is October 1 to November 30, 2026, so it
  is open now. Applicants need a German tax number and a home in Germany.
  A team elsewhere in the EU can take part by forming a German GbR. Applications may
  be in English. **Checked** (search excerpt of /en/apply; the site refused
  a direct fetch). The excerpt said up to €95,000 for six months, with a
  second stage of €63,000, and that the programme now runs until 2029.
  **Checked but unclear:** the same page mixed in dates from an earlier
  round, so these figures need confirming.
- *Shared:* open source as a condition of the money (9), small builders,
  and software for social benefit (2).
- *Friction:* only people in Germany can apply, and it funds infrastructure
  more than learning.
- *How to join in:* this matters most to German cohort members. Its
  application questions are a good model for a "what problem, for whom"
  intake.

**1.2 NLnet Foundation (Netherlands).** https://nlnet.nl
- *What it does:* It gives small grants for open internet software. The next
  deadline is November 3, 2026, at noon CET. From now on, calls close on the third day
  of odd-numbered months. The money now comes through the "Open Internet Stack",
  which follows the old Next Generation Internet programme. **Checked.**
- *The key detail:* NLnet is rewriting its generative AI policy. "Work that
  is mostly LLM-generated is already ineligible," the new version will
  require more transparency and logging, and new projects get no
  exceptions. Its advice is that anyone who plans to use LLMs should wait
  for the new policy before applying. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* open source, and credit for every tool (14).
- *Friction:* it is direct. A respected public funder of open source will
  not fund work that is mostly written by an agent. Human-shaped apps are
  built with agents, so this policy is the strongest outside test yet of
  the claim that the builder still shapes the work (13, 14).
- *How to join in:* read the new policy when it comes out, and compare it
  with the template's attribution and logging practices. If they line up,
  NLnet's terms could become a credential for the method.

**1.3 Careful Industries, and the archive of Promising Trouble (UK).**
https://www.careful.industries and https://www.promisingtrouble.net
- *What it does:* Rachel Coldicutt's studio for "inclusive innovation"
  says it wants technology to work "for 8 billion people, not 8
  billionaires". It publishes the free Careful Consequence Check, a
  report on how local communities make sense of AI (the "AI in the Street"
  pilot), and work on the risks of AI notetakers. **Checked.**
  Promising Trouble, its non-profit sister, ran Community Tech, a Community
  Tech Fellowship, and a Community Internet Handbook from 2021 to 2025. Its
  site is now an archive that sends enquiries to Careful Industries. **Checked.**
  Coldicutt sits on a sub-committee of the UK government's Digital
  Inclusion Action Committee. **Checked** (her bio in a search result).
- *Shared:* the Consequence Check is close to principle 12 (creates no new
  human-shaped problems), and community tech is close to principles 1 and 2.
- *Friction:* most of its work is consultancy for institutions, not
  individuals building things.
- *How to join in:* adapt the Consequence Check as a week-4 or week-5
  exercise, with credit. Ask Coldicutt to speak. Her 2026 Camp Digital
  talk was titled "Why the world needs hopeful technologists". **Checked.**

**1.4 Waag Futurelab and the Public Stack (Amsterdam).** https://waag.org
- *What it does:* The Public Stack is a six-dimension model for asking what
  each layer of a technology assumes, from its underlying values to how it
  places people. Waag also runs it as a workshop. Its 2025 to 2028 research
  agenda has a revised version of the model. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* principle 3, values written down as guardrails, applied to every
  layer, and principle 5, knowing whose work you build on.
- *Friction:* the model is for institutions and governments, and builders
  may find it abstract.
- *How to join in:* use the Public Stack as an optional lens in the
  "values written down" stage. Request a workshop (the contact on its
  toolkit page is Marit Hoefsloot). **Checked.**

**1.5 SUPERRR Lab (Berlin).** https://superrr.net/en
- *What it does:* feminist tech policy. It runs feministtechpolicy.org,
  published "AI Mythbusting: The Zine" in July 2026, holds Regenerative
  Feminist Tech Dinners, and keeps a German reading list on AI, power, and
  myth. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* it questions who holds power in technology, and it publishes
  open, shareable materials.
- *Friction:* its work is about policy, not building.
- *How to join in:* use the zine as a cohort reading, and hold a "tech
  dinner" as a kind of event in /events/.

**1.6 We and AI (UK).** https://weandai.org
- *What it does:* a charity that builds critical AI literacy for the people
  most affected and least consulted. It coordinates Better Images of AI, a
  free library of honest images of AI. It also runs "What AI is Not", a
  framework for educators, a campaign for public AI literacy, and
  the AI Resist List. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* literacy rather than skills training, and attention to who is
  left out. Better Images of AI could directly help the site's own pictures.
- *Friction:* it leans toward refusal and resistance, which some members may
  read as being against building with AI at all.
- *How to join in:* use Better Images of AI wherever the site needs a picture
  of AI, and offer "What AI is Not" as a reading. They take contact through
  their site.

### Africa

**1.7 Masakhane.** https://www.masakhane.io
- *What it does:* a grassroots, volunteer research community for natural
  language processing in African languages, built by Africans. As of 2020
  it had more than 1,000 participants from 30 African countries. Its values
  include Umuntu Ngumuntu Ngabantu ("a person is a person through other
  people"), African ownership, opposition to paywalls, data sovereignty,
  and kindness. It says "no NLP background is required", and it refuses
  "Parachute Research" that brings no clear value to Africa. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* learning together, open by default (9), people owning their
  data (11), and welcoming newcomers.
- *Friction:* its work is research and models, not apps. Any partnership
  has to bring real value to African communities, not just be an outreach
  line on our site.
- *How to join in:* through its Slack, its mailing list, or
  masakhane_leadership@googlegroups.com. **Checked.** Its "no parachute"
  rule is worth adopting in our own guidance for teachers who run cohorts
  in communities they do not belong to.

**1.8 Lelapa AI (Johannesburg).** https://lelapa.ai
- *What it does:* a lab founded in 2022 that builds small, efficient
  language tools for African languages. Its products are Vulavula
  (transcription and language services) and InkubaLM, a compact, openly
  available model, reported as 0.4 billion parameters, for Swahili,
  Yoruba, isiXhosa, Hausa, and isiZulu. **Checked** (press coverage, not
  its own technical report).
- *Shared:* tools sized for low bandwidth and modest devices, and built for
  the people who speak the language.
- *Friction:* it is a company.
- *How to join in:* it is an example for students whose app needs a
  language that general models handle badly.

**1.9 Data Science Africa.** https://www.datascienceafrica.org
- *What it does:* yearly summer schools and workshops held in African
  universities. DSA 2026 took place at Makerere University in Kampala in
  July 2026, on the theme "Foundational and Practical Data Science in the
  Age of Generative AI". **Checked.**
- *Shared:* it builds skills in the region, for the region.
- *Friction:* it is academic, and its students are researchers.
- *How to join in:* propose a session on the human-shaped method at the
  2027 event. Its call has not been checked.

### South and Southeast Asia

**1.10 Tattle Civic Tech (India).** https://tattle.co.in
- *What it does:* a group of technologists, researchers, and artists that
  builds tools and datasets against harmful content on Indian chat apps and
  regional-language social media. Its projects include Uli (localized
  moderation against online gender-based violence), the Deepfakes Analysis
  Unit, and Viral Spiral (a card game about media literacy). Its code is
  GPL and its text is Creative Commons. **Checked.** From memory, Uli's
  word lists came from the people it protects, and that is the best
  example here of principle 1 carried out.
- *Shared:* nearly all of the principles, especially 1, 9, and 12.
- *Friction:* very little.
- *How to join in:* it has Join Us and Collaborate pages and a mailing list.
  **Checked.** Uli would make a strong "found app" case study.

**1.11 Digital Empowerment Foundation (India).** https://defindia.org
- *What it does:* runs a network of community information and digital
  resource centres. The counts conflict: 1,000 in its 2021 plan, and 2,000
  in a later profile. In 2026 it published a framework that treats AI "not
  as top-down solutions, but as community-led enablers", built on women's
  leadership, local languages, and assisted access. It also responded to
  the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance. **Checked** (partly from a
  social post and newsletter).
- *Shared:* principle 2, and assisted access, which means a helper sits
  beside the learner, much as a cohort teacher does.
- *Friction:* most of the people it serves are beginners online, not
  builders.
- *How to join in:* its centre operators are possible future cohort leaders.
  Contact info@defindia.org. **Checked.**

**1.12 SEA-LION and AI Singapore.** https://sea-lion.ai
- *What it does:* a family of open models for Southeast Asian languages and
  cultures. It reports more than a trillion tokens across 13 regional
  languages, has a community summit, and offers guardrail models. One use
  helped migrant workers in Thailand with cross-border legal disputes.
  **Checked** (GovInsider and AI Singapore). Its launch date (2022 or 2023)
  and the details of its 2026 versions conflict between sources.
- *Shared:* human-shaped problems in local languages.
- *Friction:* it is backed by the government and is mostly about models.
- *How to join in:* it is a resource for students in that region.

### Latin America

**1.13 Laboratoria.** https://laboratoria.la/en
- *What it does:* it has trained women in Latin America for tech careers
  since 2014. It now describes its work as careers "in the AI era", with
  skills, human support, and community, and in 2024 it began to "reimagine"
  itself for an AI-transformed job market. **Checked.** The countries it
  works in and its outcome figures vary between sources.
- *Shared:* cohorts, community, and support for people who were never
  meant to code.
- *Friction:* its bootcamps lead to jobs and last five to six months.
  Human-shaped cohorts are free, short, and judge success by learning.
- *How to join in:* compare notes on running cohorts. Its alumni could
  teach Spanish-language cohorts.

**1.14 Derechos Digitales (Chile and the region).** https://www.derechosdigitales.org
- *What it does:* it has studied AI and human rights in Latin America since
  2021, especially how governments use it. It published "Latin America in
  a Glimpse: Reflections for a Community-based, Feminist AI" in January
  2025. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* community-based AI, and the voice of the global South.
- *Friction:* its work is policy and advocacy.
- *How to join in:* reading and critique. Ask for a review of the
  principles from a Latin American point of view.

**1.15 The f<a+i>r Feminist AI Research Network and the A+ Alliance.**
https://aplusalliance.org
- *What it does:* from 2021 to 2024 it funded feminist AI research and
  prototypes, with a Latin America and Caribbean hub coordinated by Paola
  Ricaurte. Its methods now continue in the Gender & AI Innovation
  Collective, which adds funded prototyping and field pilots. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* starting from research, then building a prototype, then

- *Friction:* its funding is mostly for researchers.
- *How to join in:* watch the Collective for calls. Its executive summary,
  "Incubating Feminist AI", is a good reading.

## 2. Disability-led and accessibility-first technology

**2.1 Sins Invalid's 10 Principles of Disability Justice.**
https://www.sinsinvalid.org/blog/10-principles-of-disability-justice
- *What it is:* intersectionality, leadership of those most impacted,
  anti-capitalist politic, cross-movement organizing, recognizing
  wholeness, sustainability, cross-disability solidarity, interdependence,
  collective access, and collective liberation. **Checked** (the page does
  not date or sign the list). From memory, Sins Invalid is a disability
  justice performance project in the San Francisco Bay Area, and the
  principles date from about 2015 and to Patty Berne's work.
- *Shared:* "leadership of those most impacted" is principle 1 put
  strongly. "Anti-capitalist politic" sits close to principle 2.
  "Sustainability" (pacing yourself so the work lasts) fits five-week
  cohorts and principle 10.
- *Friction:* there is little friction in values. The friction is in
  practice: a cohort is not disability-led, and quoting these principles
  without disabled builders in the room would be the "parachute" Masakhane
  warns against.
- *How to join in:* cite them on the site, with credit, as one source for
  principle 7 (tested the way people will actually use it). Invite disabled
  builders to lead a cohort.

**2.2 Inclusive Design Research Centre (IDRC) and We Count, OCAD University.**
https://idrc.ocadu.ca and https://wecount.inclusivedesign.ca
- *What it does:* Jutta Treviranus has run the IDRC since 1993. We Count is
  its work on AI and people at the edges of the data: "if only the majority
  matters ... there will always be people left marginalized". From the
  search excerpts, she chairs the committee behind Canada's standard for
  accessible and equitable AI. **Checked** (search excerpts). From memory,
  her phrase "the lawnmower of justice" describes how statistics cut away
  outliers, and the IDRC's co-design method has been adopted widely.
- *Shared:* building for one real person is how human-shaped apps start
  (principles 1 and 15). Treviranus argues that designing for the edges
  helps everyone.
- *Friction:* very little.
- *How to join in:* use We Count's free resources as a reading for the
  testing stage, and ask the IDRC to review the principles' accessibility
  claims.

**2.3 Knowbility (Austin).** https://knowbility.org
- *What it does:* a nonprofit for "equal access to technology for people
  with disabilities". AccessWorks recruits testers with disabilities for
  usability tests. AIR, the Accessibility Internet Rally, is an annual
  event that pairs volunteer web teams with nonprofits. Its training
  conference is the John Slatin AccessU, and it lists AccessU 2027 as
  upcoming. **Checked.** I could not confirm whether AccessWorks testers
  are paid.
- *Shared:* principle 7, testing with real people. AIR is very close to a
  human-shaped cohort: volunteers build for a real community group on a
  deadline.
- *Friction:* it charges for professional services.
- *How to join in:* have cohort apps take part in AIR, or recruit testers
  through AccessWorks for a week-4 testing session. Volunteering is at
  knowbility.org/about/volunteer. **Checked.**

**2.4 The A11Y Project.** https://www.a11yproject.com
- *What it does:* an open-source community site of accessibility guides and a
  checklist, built with Eleventy, that accepts contributions through
  GitHub, including "good first issues". **Checked.**
- *Shared:* open source, documentation (8), and accessibility from the
  start.
- *Friction:* none.
- *How to join in:* link its checklist from the template's testing stage.
  A student's first open-source contribution could be one of its "good
  first issues".

**2.5 Ashley Shew, *Against Technoableism* (Norton, 2023).**
- *What it is:* a short book arguing that disabled people are the experts on
  technology and disability, and that "technoableism", the belief that
  technology is the answer to disability, does harm. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* principle 1 from the other side: do not build something to
  "fix" people who never asked for it.
- *Friction:* it is a direct challenge to any eager builder in a cohort, and
  that challenge is what makes it useful.
- *How to join in:* use it as a reading, especially before any cohort app
  "for" disabled people.

**2.6 Evidence on AI coding and blind developers.**
- A 2025 study of ten visually impaired developers using an AI coding
  assistant found that it gave them more control. It also brought new
  accessibility problems and cognitive load, and they asked for "AI
  timeouts" (arXiv 2503.16491). Separate research finds that AI coding
  assistants often produce inaccessible code unless asked to make it
  accessible (CodeA11y, arXiv 2502.10884). **Checked.**
- *What it means here:* the template's agent instructions should ask for
  accessibility by default, because students will not think to ask.

## 3. Indigenous technology and data sovereignty (beyond Te Hiku)

**3.1 Local Contexts: TK Labels, BC Labels, and Notices.** https://localcontexts.org
- *What it does:* Indigenous communities set Traditional Knowledge (TK) and
  Biocultural (BC) Labels for their own material on the Local Contexts Hub.
  The Labels state the community's rules for access and use, and they can
  go on websites, datasets, publications, collection items, and samples.
  Institutions and researchers cannot apply Labels. They apply *Notices*
  (Engagement, Disclosure, and Collections Care), which mark that
  Indigenous interests exist so the community can later add its own
  Labels. Communities may translate the title of a Label but may not alter
  its icon. The text of a Notice is fixed, with an optional statement of
  use. Using Labels requires the community to decide, through its own
  authorities. **Checked.**
- *Cost:* the pages I read do not say. From memory, the Hub is free for
  communities and institutions pay a subscription, but I have not
  confirmed this.
- *The precedent:* this is the strongest model in this file for the
  human-shaped mark. (1) Only the party with authority may make a claim:
  a community applies Labels, while an outsider may only flag. (2) The
  icons are fixed and the wording can be local. (3) Each label points to a
  record on the Hub, so the claim can be traced. (4) There is a gentler
  step, the Notice, for "not yet". Principle 8's "not yet, and here is what
  I am doing about it" could have its own Notice.
- *Friction:* none in values. We must not copy the Labels' design or wording.
- *How to join in:* credit it in the brand kit's #declare section as a
  source. Contact support@localcontexts.org.

**3.2 Te Mana Raraunga, the Māori Data Sovereignty Network (Aotearoa).**
https://www.temanararaunga.maori.nz
- *What it does:* formed after a 2015 hui at Hopuhopu. Its principles,
  published in 2018, are Rangatiratanga (authority), Whakapapa
  (relationships), Whanaungatanga (obligations), Kotahitanga (collective
  benefit), Manaakitanga (reciprocity), and Kaitiakitanga (guardianship).
  Its site has a page on "Indigenous Data Sovereignty, AI & Algorithms".
  **Checked** (the six names come from the University of Auckland's
  summary).
- *Shared:* principle 11 at the scale of a people, not one person.
- *Friction:* the human-shaped principles treat data ownership as
  individual. Te Mana Raraunga's principles are collective, and a cohort
  app that holds Māori data should follow them.
- *How to join in:* link to it from principle 11's "what it means" text.
  Following the round 1 rule "translate, don't import", restate the idea in
  Ben's own words and credit the source in research notes.

**3.3 Maiam nayri Wingara Indigenous Data Sovereignty Collective (Australia).**
https://www.maiamnayriwingara.org
- *What it does:* formed in 2017 by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander
  academics. Its principles came from the Indigenous Data Sovereignty
  Summit in Canberra on June 20, 2018, and include the right of
  communities not to take part. A 2026 paper in the *Data Science Journal*
  guides organisations on putting them into practice. **Checked.**
- *Shared, friction, and how to join in:* the same as 3.2, for Australian
  cohorts.

**3.4 First Languages AI Reality (Michael Running Wolf).**
- *What it does:* an initiative to use immersive technology and AI to
  reclaim Indigenous languages, announced at a NeurIPS 2023 Indigenous in
  AI workshop. Running Wolf is its co-founder and technical director and is
  affiliated with Mila. It is not confirmed as an official Mila program.
  **Checked.** From memory, he is Northern Cheyenne and Lakota and
  co-founded Indigenous in AI.
- *Shared:* building for one's own community and its language.
- *Friction:* none known.
- *How to join in:* a possible speaker. Find the initiative's own site
  first (I did not find one).

**3.5 Context from round 1:** CARE principles, Te Hiku Media's Kaitiakitanga
License, Abundant Intelligences, the Indigenous Protocol and AI Working
Group, and Mila's Indigenous Pathfinders are covered there.

## 4. Teacher networks and K-12 practitioner communities

**4.1 National Writing Project.** https://www.nwp.org
- *What it does:* calls itself "the nation's largest network of
  teacher-leaders", with 144 local sites at universities and more than 50
  years of teachers sharing practice. NWP Online offers open communities
  of practice. Its AI work includes a 2025 webinar series with NoRedInk on
  student agency, a "Teach NWP" collection on AI, and the book *Writing
  Still Matters: Improving Student Writing in an Age of AI and Beyond*.
  **Checked.** From memory, its model is the summer institute where
  "teachers teach teachers", and that is close to how human-shaped
  teachers are meant to grow.
- *Shared:* very close. It has a "teachers teaching teachers" network model,
  values writing in your own voice (13), and calls itself teacher-led.
- *Friction:* its focus is writing, not software. Some sites are wary of AI.
- *How to join in:* the best recruiting ground for teachers who will lead
  cohorts. Ben could start with his local Writing Project site and offer a
  "write, play, publish" workshop. Writing comes first in the method, and
  that is the hook.

**4.2 Edcamp (now at Digital Promise).** https://digitalpromise.org (Edcamp
community pages)
- *What it does:* free unconferences "by educators, for educators", where
  the sessions are proposed that morning. Digital Promise took over the
  Edcamp Foundation's operations in July 2020 and keeps an organizers'
  handbook (2023 update) and a list of upcoming events. **Checked.** An
  example of the human side of AI as an unconference theme: the CIS
  Ontario Unconference, held February 9, 2026, on "how AI can elevate,
  rather than erode, the human side of learning". **Checked.**
- *Shared:* free, run by participants, and anyone can host one. That is the
  same idea as anyone being allowed to lead a cohort.
- *Friction:* none.
- *How to join in:* use the Edcamp format as a recommended kind of event on
  /events/ ("a human-shaped Edcamp", with credit and a link to the
  handbook). Bring an app showcase to a local Edcamp.

**4.3 CSTA chapters and the CSTA Responsible AI Fellowship.**
https://csteachers.org/become-a-responsible-ai-fellow/
- *What it does:* a six-month fellowship for about 15 K-12 computer science
  teachers, with a $1,000 stipend, up to 50 hours of learning, and a
  capstone. The 2026 cohort meets through December, with a Fellowship
  Summit on November 7, 2026. CSTA also has local chapters with chapter
  grants. **Checked** (the chapters appear only as navigation links).
- *Shared:* small cohorts, a capstone, and responsible AI.
- *Friction:* only CS teachers who are members can apply, and the
  fellowship is about teaching AI, not building with it.
- *How to join in:* offer a chapter meeting session. The 2026 fellows are
  natural people to invite to lead cohorts.

**4.4 aiEDU Trailblazers Fellowship.** https://www.aiedu.org/trailblazers-fellowship
- *What it does:* a paid, 12-week virtual fellowship for K-12 teachers on AI
  literacy. It has five live sessions, a showcase at the end, and an $875
  stipend, and applications reopen this fall. **Checked** (search excerpt).
- *Shared:* the cohort format and teachers trying things in class.
- *Friction:* it is about literacy, not building, and it is funded by
  industry (from memory).
- *How to join in:* compare formats, and invite alumni.

**4.5 Teach Plus Leading Edge Fellowship.** https://teachplus.org
- *What it does:* its first cohort in 2025-26 had 10 teachers learning AI
  alongside policy and communications. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* teacher voice.
- *How to join in:* invite fellows to build an app for a problem in their
  own classroom.

**4.6 Digital Harbor Foundation (Baltimore).** https://digitalharbor.org
- *What it does:* a youth maker and tech-education center in a former city
  rec center, with training for educators. **Checked** (profiles). Its
  2026 programs are not confirmed, and sources disagree on its founding
  year.
- *How to join in:* a lead only. Confirm it is still active before reaching out.

## 5. Older adults, faith and humanist groups, and youth

**5.1 Senior Planet from AARP.** https://seniorplanet.org
- *What it does:* free technology courses for people 60 and older (some
  local partners take people 50 and older), including short AI courses
  ("AI All Around", "Introduction to AI", "AI Image Generators"), an AI Hub
  community, and live sessions in libraries, churches, and centers.
  **Checked** (search excerpts; the site did not render for me).
- *Shared:* free, taught in person, and patient.
- *Friction:* it teaches people to use AI, not build with it.
- *How to join in:* a "build one small thing for your family" cohort for
  older adults would be new. Senior Planet trainers and sites are the place
  to test that idea.

**5.2 AI and Faith.** https://aiandfaith.org
- *What it does:* an interfaith network founded in 2018 in Seattle. It
  brings the values of the major religions into debate about how AI is
  built, and it reports about 200 experts from 19 countries (self-reported).
  **Checked.**
- *Shared:* human dignity, and that technology should increase what people
  can do.
- *Friction:* the site is not religious and should not become so.
- *How to join in:* context and possible readers of the principles, not
  partners.

**5.3 *Antiqua et Nova* (the Vatican, January 28, 2025), context only.**
- *What it is:* a note from the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and
  the Dicastery for Culture and Education on AI and human intelligence. It
  holds that AI should complement human intelligence and not replace it,
  and it warns about lost human skills, surveillance, and harm to
  relationships. **Checked** (publisher listings; read the Vatican's own
  text before quoting).
- *What it means here:* many church-based teachers and schools will meet AI
  through this frame. "Complement, not replace" is close to how the site
  says AI feedback never stands in for the teacher.

**5.4 Humanists UK, and the AI, Faith & Civil Society Commission.**
https://humanists.uk
- *What it does:* it has no formal position paper on AI that I could find.
  It held an AI and society panel at its 2025 Convention with its patron
  Kate Devlin, who represents Humanists UK on the AI, Faith & Civil Society
  Commission. Anil Seth gave its 2025 Voltaire Lecture on AI and
  consciousness. **Checked.** The Commission was founded in the UAE and is
  based in England, and it is also a partner of AI and Faith. **Checked.**
- *How to join in:* context only. Kate Devlin is a possible speaker.

**5.5 Encode.** https://encodeai.org
- *What it does:* founded in 2020 by Sneha Revanur, then in high school, as
  Encode Justice. Its site now describes "a small advocacy team" that
  is "pro-human pro-AI", and it lists policy wins: California SB 53, New
  York's RAISE Act, Illinois SB 315, and the TAKE IT DOWN Act. **Checked.**
  Its home page no longer describes a youth chapter model. **Checked.**
- *Friction:* it has moved from youth organizing to frontier-AI safety
  legislation, which is far from building small apps.
- *How to join in:* low fit now.

**5.6 National Youth Council of Ireland: Citizens' Youth Juries on AI.**
- *What it does:* 48 young people from across Ireland deliberated on AI and
  made recommendations. Its report came out on June 4, 2026, with the

  Ireland's 2022 National Youth Assembly on AI came before it. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* young people deciding, not just being consulted.
- *How to join in:* a model for a youth event on /events/ (a "youth jury"

  youth participation and AI in education among its priorities, which may

## 6. Precedents for a "this software is human-shaped" mark

**6.1 Local Contexts.** See 3.1. This is the best model: the person with
authority declares, the icons are fixed, each mark links to a record, and
there is a "Notice" for "not yet".

**6.2 C2PA Content Credentials.** https://c2pa.org
- *What it is:* signed metadata about where media came from. The Interim
  Trust List closed to new entries on January 1, 2026. A Conformance
  Program (launched June 2025, starting with self-assertion and two levels
  of assurance) places products that pass on a public list. Certification
  authorities set their own fees. LinkedIn, TikTok, Sony, Adobe, and
  Google's Pixel 10 are reported to sign content. **Checked** (adoption
  from a secondary analysis).
- *The lesson:* cryptographic signing is ahead of trust. Even C2PA
  started with self-assertion. A signature proves who made a claim, not
  that the claim is true.
- *Friction:* certificates and fees do not fit a $0 site. The humanshaped
  credential already uses did:web and signing (Open Badges 3.0), so the
  site does not need C2PA.

**6.3 Creative Commons and CC signals.** https://creativecommons.org
- *What it is:* CC licenses are the classic self-declared mark that became
  trusted. Each license comes in a human-readable deed, a legal code, and
  machine-readable metadata, and its icons are fixed. (The licenses are
  **from memory**, as general knowledge.) CC signals, proposed in June 2025,
  lets people who hold content say how AI may reuse it, built on
  reciprocity, recognition, and sustainability. A progress update came out
  in April 2026. **Checked.** Whether the planned November 2025 alpha
  shipped is not confirmed.
- *The lesson:* say the same thing three ways, in plain words, in full
  text, and as data. The human-shaped mark could do the same with a short
  badge, the HUMAN-SHAPED.md answers, and a JSON record.

**6.4 Contributor Covenant.** https://www.contributor-covenant.org
- *What it is:* Coraline Ada Ehmke wrote it in 2014, when codes of conduct
  were "unheard of, and very controversial". Tens of thousands of projects
  have adopted it, including the Linux kernel. **Checked** (the adoption
  counts are self-reported or secondary).
- *The lesson:* it is the closest precedent for a self-declared file in a
  repository that became a norm. It spread because adopting it took one
  file, the text was good, and it was visible in the repository. Its
  weakness is that adopting it without enforcing it was common, and
  critics noticed.
- *For us:* HUMAN-SHAPED.md is this kind of file. Its answers are what
  keeps it honest.

**6.5 IBM AI Attribution Toolkit.** https://aiattribution.github.io
- *What it is:* an experimental questionnaire that writes a standard
  statement of how AI contributed to a piece of work: what kind of system,
  what it contributed, how much humans changed it, and who is finally
  accountable. Its makers call it "a first pass" at a voluntary standard
  and admit that it works on the honor system. A CHI 2025 paper found that
  people give AI less credit than a human partner for the same
  contribution. **Checked.**
- *Shared:* principles 13 and 14 directly.
- *How to join in:* compare its fields with the template's attribution
  practice, and adopt any field we lack (such as "locus of final
  accountability").

**6.6 B Corp's 2025 standards and Fairtrade.**
- *B Corp:* the new standards launched in April 2025. They replace the old
  80-point total with required minimums across seven impact topics, so
  strength in one area can no longer make up for weakness in another, and
  they require continued improvement (some guides say milestones in years
  three and five). The EU's green-claims rules drove part of the change.
  **Checked** (the Linklaters analysis is the strongest source).
- *Fairtrade:* **from memory**. Third-party certification against published
  standards, with fees, audits, and a protected mark.
- *The lesson:* marks that let a builder offset weak spots with a total
  score lose trust. Every principle needs an honest answer, and "not yet"
  counts as honest. Human Shaped already says this; B Corp shows why it
  matters.
- *Friction:* audits and fees are the opposite of free and self-declared.

**6.7 Round 1 marks:** Not By AI and the Authors Guild's Human Authored mark
are covered there. They show the weakness of a mark that declares only the
absence of AI. A human-shaped mark declares a practice, not an absence,
which fits building with AI.

### What makes a self-declared mark trusted

The six precedents point to six features:

1. **The claim links to evidence.** Local Contexts links to the Hub record,
   CC to the license deed, and Human Shaped would link to the HUMAN-SHAPED.md answers.
2. **The person with authority makes the claim, and others can only flag.**
   Local Contexts separates Labels from Notices.
3. **The mark is fixed and the words around it can change.** The icons
   cannot be altered, but titles can be translated and statements of use
   added.
4. **"Not yet" has its own honest form.** A Notice, B Corp's improvement
   milestones, and the principles' own "not yet, and here is what I am
   doing about it".
5. **No points to offset.** B Corp's 2025 change.
6. **It can be checked, and checking is public and cheap.** Without that,
   the honor system is all there is (IBM admits as much). The site's
   public app pages and live commits already make checking cheap.
