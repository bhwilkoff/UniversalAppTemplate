## 1. Recurse Center

**Status (Checked, recurse.com):** Free, self-directed programming retreat,
6 or 12 week batches, Brooklyn or online, new batches every six weeks,
"always accepting applications." No tuition; funded by recruiting. The home
page lists "Figuring out how to code with agents?" as an example project.
3,000+ alums on Zulip.

**AI stance (Checked, blog/191, July 23, 2025, Nick Bergson-Shilcock with
Emily Bernier and Rachel Petacat):** "You should use AI-powered tools to
complement or increase your agency, not replace it." and "use it to amplify
your ambitions, not to abdicate your agency." Also: LLMs "are not good at
knowing what questions you care about, or which answers are meaningful."

**The doors**
- Localhost, public technical talks in NYC by RC community members:
  https://www.recurse.com/localhost . Next: Wed Oct 14, 2026, "RuneScape
  Bench: MMOs for Agents." The page does not say outsiders can propose talks;
  speakers are Recursers. (Checked)
- Apply as an individual: https://www.recurse.com/apply (Checked).
- Alum events calendar /calendar and Zulip are members only (Checked that
  they exist; access not tested).
- No public email or role contact on the home page, only social accounts
  (X, LinkedIn, Bluesky, etc.). (Checked)

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- A free, structured on-ramp for people not yet ready for RC's
  self-direction, and a named next step for cohort graduates who want an
  unstructured batch.
- Cohort apps and HUMAN-SHAPED.md files as worked examples of "agency, not
  replacement," which RC's post argues for but does not show in a method.
- A teaching role for Recursers: any alum can lead a cohort.

**Friction:** RC is deliberately curriculum-free and teacher-free; it is a
recruiting business; its events are for its own community. It takes no
position on software for people over profit. Partnerships with outside
programs are not advertised anywhere I found.

**First step this month:** Ben attends (or watches for the recording of) the
Oct 14 Localhost talk on agents and, after it, sends RC a short note through
a public channel citing the July 2025 post on /about/ and offering cohorts as
a place for alums to teach.

**Who:** Nick Bergson-Shilcock, cofounder (Checked as the post's author).
Public route is RC's social accounts; if Ben applies or knows a Recurser,
Zulip is the natural channel.

## 2. Civics of Technology

**Status (Checked, civicsoftechnology.org):** Research, curriculum, and
professional development so teachers and students can examine technology
critically. Five virtual conferences, all early August: 2022 to 2026 (2026 was
August 6 and 7). No 2027 date or call posted yet (Checked /conferences). Tech
Talks "currently paused" (Checked /events). The events page still shows a
June book club (Dekeyser's *Techno-Negative*); it may be stale.

**The doors**
- Guest blog post: "We welcome guest contributions to our blog!" through the
  Blog Proposal Form on https://www.civicsoftechnology.org/contact ; a pitch,
  not a draft, is enough. (Checked)
- General inquiries form on the same page; no email addresses. (Checked)
- "Seeks partners to pilot or develop curriculum, conduct research, or pursue
  other initiatives" (Checked, home page).
- Newsletter sign-up on the home page; book clubs at /book-clubs (discussion on
  Bluesky with #CivicsOfTech); meet-ups at /meet-ups. (Checked that pages are
  linked.)
- 2027 conference: if it follows five years of pattern, early August 2027,
  virtual; the call would likely come in spring (pattern inferred, not posted).

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- An honest test case: a course that uses AI agents and writes its values down
  as guardrails. Their curriculum asks "what does this technology do to us";
  a cohort can answer that question about its own app in public.
- A guest post that faces the disagreement, for example "What would it take
  for AI-built software to create no new human problems?", with cohort apps
  as evidence, not promises.
- Pilot their technoskeptical lessons inside Cohort Prep or week 1, and credit
  them (principle 5).

**Friction:** The strongest. Much of this community favors refusal or
resistance to generative AI in schools (2026 theme "Against Tech Hype"; the
book club reading is on refusing the machine). Expect pushback on dependence on
frontier labs. The audience is K-12 and teacher education, not adult builders.

**First step this month:** Ben submits a blog pitch through the Blog Proposal
Form that names the disagreement plainly and offers a cohort as a place to try
their lessons.

**Who:** Dan Krutka, Marie Heath, Jacob Pleasants, conference organizers
(Checked in round 1, 2026-10-08). Contact only through the site's forms.

## 3. Data & Society, AI Civics

**Status (Checked, datasociety.net/news-events announcement, May 12, 2026):**
Two-year public education program, $2 million from Humanity AI. Inaugural
partner: Digital Public Library of America. Begins with libraries, then
"labor organizations, faith groups, educational communities, and others."
Goal: "lay groundwork for a national civic coalition." It grew out of a fall
2025 "Understanding AI" series with NYPL. Led by Dr. Meg Young, Program
Director, AI Civics (Checked, her profile). A senior researcher job posting
(Search, applications closed July 30, 2026) describes co-created toolkits,
facilitation guides, and a train-the-trainer model. The /research/ai-civics/
URL returned 404 today; the old announcement URL also 404s.

**The doors**
- Expression-of-interest form for organizations:
  https://mailchi.mp/datasociety/ai-civics (Checked, linked from the
  announcement). This is the one stated door.
- General D&S newsletter (Checked, linked on Meg Young's profile).
- D&S public events (round 1 listed an Oct 7, 2026 book talk; not rechecked).

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- "Educational communities" are a named future sector. Human Shaped is an
  educational community already running a many-teachers, free model, close to
  train-the-trainer.
- A building track for civics: once people know how to influence AI decisions
  in their town, a cohort is how a library or group builds the small tool it
  actually needs, with values written down first.
- Cohort apps as cases for their toolkits, and the principles and
  HUMAN-SHAPED.md as a classroom artifact anyone can adapt.

**Friction:** AI Civics is about democratic power over AI used on people,
not about building with AI; D&S researchers will ask about dependency on
frontier labs and on GitHub. They are funded and starting with libraries, so
a small volunteer project may wait for the later sectors. Their scope is US.

**First step this month:** Ben fills in the AI Civics interest form, naming
Human Shaped as an educational community with volunteer teachers and offering
a library-ready cohort as a pilot.

**Who:** Meg Young, Program Director, AI Civics (Checked). Use the form, not
a guessed address.

## 4. Collective Intelligence Project (Weval, Global Dialogues)

**Status (Checked, cip.org):** Projects listed: Weval, Global Dialogues,
Community Models, Jhilmil, Roadmap for Democratic AI, Alignment Assemblies.
Weval (Checked, weval.org) is active: evals dated as late as Oct 1, 2026,
"1,000+ contributors," education evals already there ("AI Tutoring Best
Practices," "Student Homework Help Heuristics"). No coding-agent eval listed.
The Global Dialogues Challenge (2025) is finished, with winners posted
(Checked /challenge).

**The doors**
- Weval: build a blueprint in the browser sandbox (https://weval.org/sandbox,
  linked as "Submit an evaluation") or as YAML/JSON in the public repo
  github.com/weval-org/configs, released CC0. Rubrics use `should` and
  `should_not` points with LLM judges. (Checked, docs/METHODOLOGY.md)
  Multi-turn conversations are not described in the methodology doc.
- Global Dialogues for organizations (Checked, cip.org/globaldialogues):
  "Rideshare questions" (submit a question for a launch; CIP helps shape it),
  a benchmarking coalition, topic-specific dialogues, building on the open data.
  Contact hi@cip.org, subject "Global Dialogues Partnership."
- Community Models: cm.cip.org (Checked that it is linked).
- General contact hi@cip.org (Checked); newsletter on the home page.

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- A Weval blueprint, written in a cohort, that tests whether an AI agent
  follows the human-shaped method when it reviews a student's work: does it
  ask the method's questions instead of answering, does it label itself as AI,
  does it leave the decision with the student, does it refuse to stand in for
  the teacher. That is a public, CC0 contribution in a domain Weval lacks.
- Rideshare questions for Global Dialogues about learning to build with AI,
  for example "Have you made software for yourself with AI, and what did it
  change?"
- A Community Model experiment using PRINCIPLES.md as the constitution.

**Friction:** Low on stance; CIP works with frontier labs. Scope is AI
governance, not teaching. Weval evaluates single prompts most easily, so the
method's back-and-forth may have to be reduced to single turns.

**First step this month:** Ben (or a student) drafts a small Weval blueprint
in the sandbox with five prompts and `should`/`should_not` points drawn from
the review skill, then opens a pull request to weval-org/configs.

**Who:** Divya Siddarth, co-founder and executive director; Evan Hadfield,
projects lead (Checked in round 1, cip.org/team). Public address hi@cip.org.

## 6. The Resonant Computing Manifesto

**Status (Checked, resonantcomputing.org):** Five principles: Private,
Dedicated, Plural, Adaptable, Prosocial. Page last updated November 18, 2025
(changelog replaced "user" with people-centered language). Long signatory
list, no count shown. Signatory names and the Dec 5, 2025 launch are from
round 1 (Search).

**The doors**
- Sign: the Google Form linked on the page (Checked).
- Theses document: an open Google Doc of specific claims, open for
  contributions (Checked).
- Resonant Computing Lab (Checked, resonantcomputinglab.com), run by lab
  directors Carson Levine and Liz Dorman, with three programs:
  - **Gardens:** 3 to 10 people on one question for a season, aiming at a
    framework, paper, or prototype. You can propose one.
  - **Gatherings:** community-hosted dinners, talks, walks, work nights;
    selected ones go in a monthly list of aligned events. You can submit one.
  - **Projects:** public recognition and a badge for resonant tools and
    grassroots experiments, sometimes small grants. You can nominate work.

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- Cohort apps as grassroots resonant projects (a nomination per cohort).
- Cohort sessions or show-your-work nights listed as Gatherings.
- A Garden proposal on "how ordinary people learn to build resonant software
  with AI," with the course as the prototype.
- A thesis for the shared document on teaching non-programmers.

**Friction:** Low on values. The audience is professional technologists and
investors; the Lab has patron capital, so keep Human Shaped's free, $0 stance
clear. A badge program overlaps with the "declare your software
human-shaped" mark; position them as companions, not rivals.

**First step this month:** Ben signs the manifesto and submits the next
cohort's final show-your-work session as a Gathering for the monthly list.

**Who:** Alex Komoroske, lead instigator (Search, round 1); Carson Levine and
Liz Dorman, Lab directors (Checked). The Lab page lists their addresses for
funding inquiries only; use the Lab's propose/share/nominate forms instead.

## 10. Raspberry Pi Foundation's AI education (Experience AI, the new LLM unit)

**Status (Checked)**
- Experience AI (experience-ai.org): free lessons for secondary classrooms, a
  free three-week course "Understanding AI for educators," built by the
  Raspberry Pi Foundation and Google DeepMind; "more than 180 countries";
  2025 UNESCO King Hamad Bin Isa Al-Khalifa Prize.
- New unit (blog, Sept 28, 2026, Rehana Al-Soltane): "Using LLMs strategically
  for learning," five lessons, ages 13 to 16, part of Experience AI with
  Google DeepMind. Teaches "feedback literacy" in three kinds: telling
  (giving the answer), guiding (hints), and challenging (questions that make
  learners think harder). Piloted with educators in South Africa, Nigeria,
  Kenya, the UK, India, and elsewhere. No call for partners in the post.
- Partner network (experience-ai.org/en/partners): "Register your interest"
  link for organizations; partners give local expertise, translation, and
  teacher training. Eligibility not stated.
- Code Club (codeclub.org): free for school-aged young people; adults can run
  a club with no coding experience, or volunteer at one.
- Hello World magazine: I could not find a current author call this session
  (Search only, results from 2017 and 2018).

**What Human Shaped could offer**
- The adult counterpart to their unit: the review skill already asks
  questions (their "challenging" feedback) instead of giving answers. Human
  Shaped can cite the telling/guiding/challenging framework, with credit, and
  report how adults respond to it with coding agents.
- Volunteers: cohort teachers who want to work with young people can run or
  help at a Code Club and bring Experience AI lessons into it.
- An adult-learner case for their research team (they run a research
  seminar series, From memory).

**Friction:** Children and schools, not adults building real apps. Google
DeepMind partnership. Colligan's June 2025 post argues kids still need to
learn to code by hand, which a course built around agents should answer
honestly. The partner network is for organizations that serve young people
in their own countries.

**First step this month:** Ben adds a credited reference to the "telling,
guiding, challenging" framework in the review skill's notes and sends the
unit's author a short thank-you through the Foundation's public channels
saying how an adult course is using it.

**Who:** Rehana Al-Soltane, author of the unit post (Checked); Philip
Colligan, CEO (Checked in round 1 as author). Experience AI partner team
through the "Register your interest" link.
