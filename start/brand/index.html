The brand kit, free to use.

These are the files that make a flyer, a slide, or a website look like it belongs to the same movement: the wordmark, the icon, the names for meetups and hackathons, and the two marks for declaring an app human-shaped. Every letter in them is drawn as an outline, so they look right anywhere, even where the typeface is not installed.

These words are a draft, written with an AI agent, and Ben has not read them yet. The files are drawn from the same shapes as the site's own wordmark and icon.

The wordmark.

The name is two words joined by an arch where the hyphen would be, so that it reads as "human-shaped" and as the top of a person's head at the same time. Use the light version on light backgrounds and the dark version on dark ones, and use the one-color versions when something is printed in a single ink, like a flyer that will be photocopied. The PNG copies are for slide tools that will not take an SVG file.

The icon.

The icon is the arch on its own, sitting high in a clay square with the empty space below it, so that it reads as the top of a head and never as a frown. Use it where the whole name will not fit, like a profile picture, a sticker, or the corner of a slide.

Backgrounds for calls.

When you teach a cohort or join one, you can put one of these behind you in Google Meet, so that the call feels like it belongs to the same place as the site. The middle of each one is empty on purpose, because that is where Meet puts you, and the wordmark and the arch stay up and out of the way. In Meet, press Backgrounds and effects under your own video (or Video settings once you are in the call), and under Blur and personal backgrounds, choose Add your own personal background and pick the file you saved. Your cohort's page can also make one with your cohort's name on it.

Names for meetups and hackathons.

When you run a Human-Shaped Meetup or a Human-Shaped Hackathon, put its name on your flyer, your slides, and the sign on the door with these. You do not need to ask anyone first, as long as the principles are in front of everyone and a hackathon keeps the five things its kit names. Please do not suggest that Ben or humanshaped.org is running your event unless they are.

The marks for your software.

These two are not for events. They belong on software whose builders have written a HUMAN-SHAPED.md, and each one links to that file, so that anyone who sees the mark can read the answers behind it. Showing that your software is human-shaped says which one is yours, with code to paste.

The colors.

There is one accent, and it is used sparingly: clay for the arch and for links, and everything else in ink on paper. Clay on paper has a contrast of about 5.9 to 1, and ink on paper about 15.6 to 1, so both clear the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines' bar of 4.5 to 1 for ordinary text, and the dark versions clear it too.

The typeface.

Everything is set in Commissioner, designed by Kostas Bartsokas and shared under the SIL Open Font License, so it is free to install and use. The wordmark and the headings use a weight of 680, labels use 600, and reading text uses 400.

Using them well.

People only learn to recognize a mark when it looks the same everywhere they see it, so these few rules are about keeping it the same.

  1. The arch is the hyphen between "human" and "shaped", or it sits high in its own space, never centered low. Centered low, it reads as a frown, and high, it reads as the top of a head. If you make something new with the arch alone, put the empty space below it, the way the icon does.
  2. Give it room. Leave clear space around the wordmark at least as tall as its letter "n" on every side, so that nothing crowds it. The dashed line below shows how much.
  3. Keep it large enough to read: the wordmark at least 100 pixels wide on a screen and 25 millimeters wide in print, and the icon at least 16 pixels.
  4. Don't recolor it, stretch it, rebuild it in another typeface, or add shadows or outlines, and don't set it over a busy photo. If none of the versions here suits your background, use whichever one-color version has the most contrast with it.
  5. Write the name the same way, too: "Human Shaped" for this site and the community, "human-shaped" when it describes software, and "Human-Shaped" in the names of things, like the Human-Shaped Principles, a Human-Shaped Meetup, and a Human-Shaped Hackathon.

Where the files come from.

Every file on this page is drawn by a script from Commissioner itself (make_brand.py for the wordmark, the icon, and the event names, make_mark.py for the marks, and make_backgrounds.py for the backgrounds), so if the wordmark ever changes, the files are drawn again rather than edited by hand. Make something with them, and show us in the discussions.
