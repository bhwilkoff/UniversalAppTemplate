Start something

Bring it to a room of your own.

The ideas are free to carry anywhere. This is what you need in order to say your own app is human-shaped, to gather people for a conversation about it, or to spend a day building this way, so that you can start something wherever you are.

Declare your software human-shaped.

The Human-Shaped Principles are a short list of plain statements that an app can meet. Declaring yours means saying, in writing and in public, how your app meets each one, and the declaration lives in your own repository where anyone can check it.

  1. Read the principles against your own app, and notice the ones it does not meet yet.
  2. Copy the declaration template into your repository as HUMAN-SHAPED.md.
  3. For each principle, say whether your app meets it, does not meet it yet, or does not apply, in a sentence or two of your own, with a link to evidence someone else can open. Your agent can help you find the evidence, and the sentences are yours to write.
  4. Mark it working toward until every principle is met or does not apply, and then mark it declared. Working toward is an honest place to be.

The mark, for your README and your site.

Once the declaration is in your repository, you can show it wherever people meet your app, in its README on GitHub or at the foot of its website. The mark is a link to your own HUMAN-SHAPED.md, so anyone who wonders what it means can read your answers and the evidence behind them, which is the only thing that makes a mark like this worth trusting.

Use the working toward mark until your file says declared, and then switch. Please keep its colors and its shape as they are, because people only learn to recognize a mark when it looks the same everywhere they see it.

Paste this where you want the mark to appear, and change the first address to your own declaration. It follows the reader's light or dark setting on GitHub and on most websites. For the declared mark, change working-toward to declared in both image addresses, and change the alt text to match.

Start a meetup.

A meetup can be an evening at a library, a classroom, or a kitchen table, and it needs nothing more than a room, a screen, and a few people with apps they wish existed. Nobody has to have written code.

  1. Read Computer-shaped problems together, and credit the Bluesky post the phrase came from.
  2. Ask each person to pick one feature they want in an app they use, and name its shape: what the computer can carry, and what the person has to keep.
  3. Read one real prompt from Talking to your agent, and talk about what the builder wanted, what the agent got wrong, and how the prompt fixed it.
  4. End with each person bringing back one question, and post the best of them in the discussions so the next room can start from them.

Run a Human-Shaped Hackathon.

A Human-Shaped Hackathon is a day of building in which what people learn is what counts as success, and every person builds one real app of their own for people they can name. Ask everyone to arrive with a GitHub account, an AI agent they can use and a computer, and to bring the problem rather than the solution.

  1. Put the four questions on the wall where everyone can see them: does it deepen understanding, does it invite participation, does it support human agency, and is it clear rather than clever?
  2. Start everyone at stage 00, writing why their app exists and who it is for before the agent writes any code, and naming one line the app will never cross.
  3. Build toward stage 01: one platform, with real data, open on the builder's own phone.
  4. Close with a show-and-tell where each person shows what they built and one decision their values changed, because a value that never changed a decision is decoration.

Talk about it with everyone else.

The humanshaped organization on GitHub holds every conversation about this work, and its discussions are open to anyone. Tell people about the meetup you are planning, ask for a second pair of eyes on a declaration, or show what your hackathon made.
