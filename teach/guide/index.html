Leader's guide

The steps link to your cohort once you lead one. Plan a cohort

A cohort

Length
Five weeks, after a prep week
Meets
Weekly on Google Meet, for 60 or 75 minutes
Groups
Groups of three, once there are eight learners
Calls hosted by
meet@humanshaped.org, one calendar event per session
Leaders
One or more per cohort

Your work is running the sessions, reading the checks, and noticing who needs a word. Nobody needs to be an expert in building apps to lead.

The course in full

Before the first session

  1. Co-lead one session firstPeople, in their cohort

    A first session goes best when it already feels familiar. Ask a leader to add you to their cohort, and take the parts of one session you are most comfortable with.

  2. Set the day and timeSettings

    The day, time, start date, and length that every session is built from. People see them on Open cohorts before they join, each on their own clock.

  3. Make the sessionsSessions

    A prep session and one for each week, each starting from the course’s plan at your session’s length. Each becomes its own calendar event with its own Meet link, sent to everyone in the cohort within the hour, and to each person who joins later.

  4. Make the GitHub repositoryConversation

    The cohort’s shared space: a private repository in the humanshaped organization that holds its conversation, its Try my app board, and its files. One button makes it, with a team of the cohort’s people, a README, and a safeguard that keeps a copy of anything a push writes over. Learners are added as they join.

  5. Watch the repositoryConversation

    Watching it on GitHub means GitHub emails you about new conversations, requests to try an app, and files, by your own settings. Each learner can do the same from Conversation.

  6. Open for joiningThis week

    The cohort appears on Open cohorts and people can join. Until then, only its leaders can see it. People you invited from a time poll get an email from meet@humanshaped.org with a link that joins them.

  7. Read each session’s planSessions

    Each session’s page holds its plan, with every part’s words in plain view and your own notes. Change anything there before it runs; learners read the same plan without your notes.

  8. Make the groups, from eight learnersPeople

    With eight learners or more, groups of three stay together all five weeks, so someone is there to show to when one person is away. Below eight, everyone shows their work to the whole call.

  9. Check each learner’s place in the repositoryPeople

    Learners add files and post by default, and leaders manage the repository. If someone needs less, choose Read only, or Read, and sort asks, beside their name, and GitHub changes within seconds. Every change there can be undone from its history.

  10. Connect your agent (optional)Connect

    Your own Claude or Gemini can read the cohort with you: the roster, each learner’s work across the weeks, and the class as it stands right now. It only reads, and it never speaks for you.

Each session

Join the call from its calendar event, signed in as meet@humanshaped.org, and open the Human Shaped add-on from Meet’s Activities. The add-on holds the plan you prepared: press Start the session, move from part to part, ask the checks, and see who is waiting to show their work, all without leaving the call. Learners open the same panel from the session’s page until the add-on is listed for everyone. The session’s thread opens in Conversation on its own the day before.

Each week’s minutes and words

The four parts

  1. Bring backThe largest part

    Each person shows the stage’s “Be ready to...” on the device it runs on and explains one decision with the agent closed. The others ask one question to understand it, then answer the three questions: where is it going, how is it going, and what is next. In groups from eight learners; below that, to the whole call.

  2. One thing that unblocks the next stageTogether

    A few minutes from the next stage’s own text, then everyone tries it on their own app, often on the board.

  3. StartBefore leaving

    Everyone sends the first prompt of the next stage to their agent, so they leave with the week already begun.

  4. The checkThe last minutes

    Each learner answers two questions privately: the week’s question, and “What is still muddy?” You read every answer before the next session.

Between sessions

  1. Read the checks and say what changesSessions

    Read every answer before the next session, since they show who understood the week and who needs a word. What you write shows as people arrive next time.

  2. Answer feedback and questionsFeedback

    Learners ask for feedback on what they built, or ask a question. Each waits here, oldest first, until a leader answers it.

  3. Write each learner a follow-upSessions

    Follow-up gathers what each person did in the session (what they showed, asked, and answered) with a place to write them a note. A note stays in your browser until you send it.

  4. Write to anyone who missed the sessionSessions

    The course asks for a private note within two days, with an easy way back in and an easy way out. Follow-up lists who has not been seen since the week began, and opens your own email for anyone who allows it.

  5. Look at Try my appTry my app

    Who has asked for their app to be tried on a device they do not have. In weeks 3 and 4, make sure each request finds someone, and take one yourself if it waits.

  6. Put the week’s materials in FilesFiles

    Slides, readings, and examples go in that week’s folder, so the cohort finds them in one place and every version is kept.

  7. Prepare next week’s sessionSessions

    On its page, adjust its parts, their words, and the check to what you heard.

When the cohort finishes

  1. Mark the cohort finishedSettings

    The people who stayed can still read the cohort. Each session’s questions and answers are deleted, and credentials can be recorded.

  2. Record each credential that was earnedPeople

    Open each person’s repository and every link first. A signer then signs the credential, and anyone can check it at its own address.

When someone is stuck

About twenty minutes of trying, then their group, then you. When you help, ask what they have already tried before suggesting anything.
