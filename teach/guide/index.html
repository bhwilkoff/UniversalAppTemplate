Teaching

Leading a cohort of your own.

I want the cohorts to reach more people than one teacher ever could, so anyone who wants to lead one can ask to, and because the cohorts are free, what a teacher gives is time and attention rather than a fee. This page says what leading a cohort asks of you, what you need before the first session, and how to ask.

What it asks of you.

A cohort runs for about five weeks, with a week of setup before them and one live session every week on Google Meet, at a day and time you choose. Each session has the same three parts in the same order (people show what they brought back, everyone reads one real prompt together, and then everyone builds), and every one has moments to stop and check what people understood, where what they explain back is the decisions they made rather than the code the agent wrote.

Between sessions, people ask you for feedback on what they built and ask you questions, and each one waits on your teaching page until you answer it, oldest first, so nobody's request sinks under a newer one. You also set the cohort's groups (pairs, trios, or small groups, easy to change), and you write each week's challenge in your own words.

What you need before the first session.

Time, first of all: five weekly sessions, plus the hours between them to read what people share and answer it.

A GitHub account, because it is how you sign in here, where your students' apps live, and where your cohort talks. Each cohort's conversation is a private repository in the humanshaped organization, reached through a secret team of the cohort's people, and since only the organization's owners can make those, for now I make them for each cohort and you name them on your teaching page.

A Google account to host from.

Only the host needs one, because your students join from whatever account they already have. I host my own cohorts from meet@humanshaped.org, an account set aside for sessions so that nothing depends on my personal login, and an account of your own set aside the same way does the same for you.

The hub never pays for a seat, which keeps it free to run, and the cost of that choice falls on the host: a free personal Google account ends group calls at 60 minutes and has no breakout rooms. So either keep your sessions within the hour, or host from an account you already have through work or school, once you have checked that it allows longer calls.

The weekly event and its Meet link.

Once you have made the weekly sessions on your teaching page, each week needs its Meet link. The simplest way is one recurring Calendar event, made by hand from your host account with a Meet link, which keeps the same link every week. The Meet events script does the same work and checks it for you, though it was written for meet@humanshaped.org and has not yet been run by another host. Either way, put the link in each week on your teaching page, and your cohort's pages will show it.

Sessions are recorded so that missing a week never means falling behind, and the session recorder is a free Chrome extension that only the host installs. It was built around meet@humanshaped.org too, and making it work for other hosts is still to come.

A card beside you, with OBS.

Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.

The simplest way to put a card in front of everyone is to open the card page from your cohort's live page and present that tab in Meet, which needs nothing installed. Presenting takes your face off the main view, though, and when I want people to see me and the card at once, I make my camera into a scene with OBS Studio, which is free, and send that scene to Meet as if it were my camera.

  1. Open your cohort's card page in its own Chrome window, and make the window narrow and tall, about a third of your screen, since the card page arranges itself for whatever width it has.
  2. In OBS, press the + under Sources, add a Video Capture Device for your camera, and drag its red handles so that you fill the left two thirds of the canvas.
  3. Press + again and add the card's window: on Windows, Linux, and older Macs it is Window Capture, and on macOS 13 or later it is macOS Screen Capture with that one window chosen. Place it in the right third, and hold Alt (Option on a Mac) while dragging an edge to crop away the window's frame.
  4. Press Start Virtual Camera in the Controls dock.
  5. In Meet, open Video settings and choose the OBS camera instead of your own. Leave Meet's own backgrounds and effects off, because they would treat the card as part of the room behind you.

The card page follows what you send from the live page, so you change the card there and the scene changes with it. Two things I have not checked yet: the exact name the OBS camera has in Meet's list, and whether OBS's own Browser Source can stay signed in to the hub, which would let you skip the separate window. On a Mac, OBS will also ask for permission to record the screen the first time.

Asking to teach.

Teaching here is by invitation while the first cohorts find their shape, so leading one starts with a short request: why you would like to teach, and what you would bring. I read every request myself, and my answer appears on the same page where you asked.
