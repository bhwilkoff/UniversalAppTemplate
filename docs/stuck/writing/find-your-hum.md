# I cannot find my hum

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review.*

When I wrote about human-shaped problems, I described the thing you are
looking for as "the gentle hum of worry or concern that you have about
an area of your life," something "that has gone unsolved for months or
even years," and most of all "something that you cannot do yourself"
(my piece, kept word for word in the site's DECISIONS.md under
"Human-shaped problems," October 6, 2026). A hum is quiet by nature, so
not finding it on the first night is normal, and the way to find it is
to listen for what you already keep saying.

**Ask yourself first.** What have I complained about more than twice
this year, to anyone, about any part of my life?

**Try this.** Write a list of ten before you open the agent, and do not
judge them yet. Then cross out every one that a spreadsheet, a search,
or a better price could solve, because those are computer-shaped: they
see the world as "disparate data points to be aggregated, quantified,
and leveraged," and a human-shaped problem sees it as "interrelated
stories to be told, experienced, and added to" (the same piece). Then
open your agent and use it as a listener, not as an author:

> Here is a list of things that bother me. Do not suggest apps yet. For
> each one, ask me one question about who else it touches and how long
> it has been going on.

Answer its questions in your own words, and notice which answers made
you want to keep talking. That is usually the hum.

**From my apps.** None of my four apps began as an idea for an app.
Bsky Dreams began with Bluesky feeling unfinished to me, and BOBA
Playbook began with a card game I wanted to understand more deeply
rather than have something think for me (Bsky-Dreams README, "Why Build
Another Client?"; BOBA-Playbook PITCH.md; both read in
[research note 01](../../research/curriculum/01-mining-bens-process.md#1-living-with-the-hum)).
In both cases the hum came first and lived with me for a long time
before there was anything to build.

**In any tool.** This page is the same in Claude Code, Antigravity, and
the open path, because the work happens in your own words and the agent
only asks questions.

**Go deeper.** Read [computer-shaped problems](../../human-shaped/computer-shaped-problems.md)
and take one of your crossed-out items through it: write the
human-shaped version of the same wish, the way my piece sets its good
human-shaped problems beside its computer-shaped ones.

**Who to ask.** Your trio, who often hear the hum in how you talk before
you do, and then your teacher, because choosing the problem is a
decision no agent can make for you.

Ten complaints, one cross-out pass, and one conversation.
