# Cohort Prep: getting set up and meeting your group

**Where the cohort is.** Everyone has spent the week on [Getting set
up](../path/setup.md), which counts nine steps on a Mac from "no GitHub
account" to "my app is live on my phone", and only a few of them are
things a person does with their own hands. Some people will arrive with
their app's address on their phone. Some will arrive stuck on a sign-in
that did not finish. Both are fine.

This session is different from the five that follow. Nobody shows a
stage's work yet, and nobody is asked what they think of anyone else's
app, because people need to know who is in the room before they will
show it something unfinished. So, this session is for getting everyone
unstuck, meeting their group, hearing how we talk to one another, and
seeing what a good bring-back looks like before anyone has to make one.

**Know who is in the room first.**

## The move and the question

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (curriculum milestone C5, October 7, 2026).*

**Publish.** This session leads with publishing, before anything is built: everyone opens the app's address on their own phone and shows it to two people they have only just met. Writing starts at the end, with the first hums.

The question the whole session turns on: **What do I need in order to build at all?**

Every session is one round of Write, Play, Publish at a larger size,
the same three moves as stage 00, and the principles decide what gets
written, played with, and published
([`00-curriculum-design.md`](../research/curriculum/00-curriculum-design.md)).

## The run of show

| Minutes (75) | Scene | Kind | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | Arrive | Talk | Your name, and in one sentence, what you want your app to do for someone you know. |
| 10-18 | How we talk to one another | Talk | The four rules, said aloud. And one true story about a rough first week. |
| 18-38 | Meet your group | Rehearsal rooms | Groups of three. The room's prompt: "Show your app's address on your phone, and say one thing that hums in your life." |
| 38-41 | Break | Break |  |
| 41-59 | A strong bring-back and a weak one | Design stage | Both shown, then everyone practices the three questions on the weak one, on the board. On the board: *The three questions*. |
| 59-70 | Start | Reflection | Everyone begins stage 00 and sends its first prompt. Then: "What has hummed in your life for months? Write three before you open the agent." |
| 70-73 | Check for understanding | Question | "What do you hope this app will do for someone?" (in their own words) |
| 73-75 | How ready do you feel? | Question | "How ready do you feel to build?" (a scale) |

**In the rehearsal rooms,** each builder's turn runs these scenes, 6 minutes in all: show the address on your phone (3), one thing that hums in your life (2), one question to understand it (1). The room's prompt is on screen the whole time.

These are the scenes a new cohort is made with on humanshaped.org, from
[`runs-of-show.json`](runs-of-show.json), and every one of them can be
changed in the class builder, before the session and during it. **At 60 minutes,** give Arrive 8, the rules 7, the groups 15, the board 15, Start 10, and the check 5, with no break. **At 90,** give Arrive 10, the rules 8, the groups 25, a five-minute break, the board 22, Start 15, and the check 5.

**What to watch for tonight.**

- Anyone whose setup is still stuck. Bring them to the main room and work through it where others can watch.
- Anyone who has not met their trio yet.

## The opening

> Welcome. Before anything else, I want you to know what the next six
> weeks ask of you: one app of your own, built with your agent, and
> shown each week to two other people who are building theirs. Tonight
> nobody shows a stage, and nobody gives anyone feedback. We are here to
> get everyone set up, to meet your group, and to see what a good
> bring-back looks like before you have to make one. Cameras are
> welcome and never required, and the chat counts as being here. Let's
> start with names, and one sentence each: what do you want your app to
> do for someone you know?

## How we talk to one another

Read the four rules from `COURSE.md` aloud, then say why they are
there. Something like this:

> These are small things, and they are the things that make people stop
> showing unfinished work. All of us will break one of them sooner or
> later, me included. When it happens, someone says so, the person who
> broke it says thanks, and we keep going.

Then tell one true story about how rough a first week is. If you have
built an app with an agent yourself, tell your own. If not, tell the
one from `COURSE.md`: on Archive Watch's third day, the commits run
from "UI feedback pass" to "UI feedback round 7", each round a list of
what was still wrong. The point to land is that a long list of what is
wrong is what the method looks like when it is working.

## What they bring back

The bring-back for this week is the last line of Getting set up:

> Be ready to open your app's address on your phone, and to say in one
> sentence what you want it to do for someone you know.

**The bar.** "You have your own copy of the template on GitHub, an agent
that can read and change it, and your app's name at its own address."

**What to look for.** The address opens on the phone, not the laptop,
and shows the app's name. The sentence names a person or a group the
student actually knows, and says what the app does for them rather than
listing features. A student whose address only works on the computer,
or whose sentence is about "users," is not yet there, and the main room
is where that gets fixed tonight.

## A strong bring-back and a weak one

This is the design stage scene. Put the board on the main stage from the
*The three questions* template, so everyone writes their three answers
for the weak example where the others can see them.

Show both, on screen, for half of what week 1 will ask: stage 00's "Be
ready to share your 'why we build' paragraph, the people outside your
screen, and your one rule, and to explain why you drew that line where
you did and what it will cost you."

**The strong one** is built from Archive Watch, from stage 00 itself.
The rule is "No AI-written lists or copy in the product," dated, and in
my own words: "I don't want AI making lists and writing copy. Any time we
can use metadata or user copy/categorization from archive.org." The
people outside the screen are named: the people who uploaded and
reviewed each film on archive.org, and the people on screen in the
films. The line was drawn there because a model could write ten
thousand blurbs in an afternoon, and what it would cost is a pipeline to
build and keep running instead.

**The weak one** I wrote myself, for the same app, so that it belongs to
nobody: "This is Archive Watch. It's for people who like old movies. My
rule is no AI. It's going pretty well."

Put everyone in pairs for three minutes to answer the three questions
about the weak one: where is it going, how is it going, and what is
next. Then ask the whole room what they could not answer, and why. The
answers people reach for are the reasons the strong one is strong: it
names who the app touches, it draws a line with a reason, and it says
what holding that line costs.

## Start

Everyone opens their note and begins [stage 00](../path/00-why-we-build.md):
the human-shaped problem and the why at the top, then its second step, telling the agent why in
their own words and asking it to rewrite the "Why we build" paragraph in
`AGENTS.md`. Everyone sends that prompt before they leave.

## The check

The question scenes at the end of the run of show, answered privately, and
shown on the main stage without names only if you choose:

1. **What do you hope this app will do for someone?** (in their own words).
2. **How ready do you feel to build?** (a scale), of 5, from "Not ready yet" to "Ready to build".

**Also prepared,** from this guide's first version, for when you want the stage's own question instead: "What did your agent do for you during setup that you expected to have to do yourself?"

That question is the stage's own lesson ("You make the accounts. The agent
does the rest.") turned into a question, and the answers tell you who has
already started to hand the agent work and who is still doing it all by
hand.

## The task, and its evidence

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (curriculum milestone C5, October 7, 2026).*

**The task.** Set up, and open the app's address on your phone. Begin your list of hums.

**Evidence a person can open.** The repository, and the address open on a phone.

**On the credential.** The repository is the credential's first evidence. Students do the thing itself, and what they
make is the evidence; nobody writes a report about it
([`05-inquiry-and-evidence.md`](../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md)).

## When someone is stuck

Every entry in the [stuck library](../stuck/README.md) starts with a
question to ask yourself, then what to try, then who to ask. This week,
point people to:

- [Stuck playing](../stuck/playing/README.md), for when the agent fixes what you cannot see, a bug keeps returning, or the tools themselves are stuck.

And the ladder for any of them: name the kind of stuck, write three
lines (what you tried, what you expected, what you saw), ask the agent
for a way to understand it rather than the fix, look on the device and
at the last thing that worked, then your trio, then the teacher. About
twenty minutes before the trio is a default each person can move.

## The close

> Before you go, answer the two questions on the live page. Only I will
> see them, and I will read every one before next week. This week you
> will write down why your app exists and get a first version of it
> running on real data, and next week you will show it to your group.
> It will not be finished, and it is not supposed to be.

## After the session

Read the checks and write your two sentences for next week's opening.
Reach out within two days to anyone who missed the session or left
without their address on their phone, and offer them half an hour
before week 1. Then confirm the groups of three, and send each group
its names, so everyone knows who they will be showing next week.
