# Week 3: proof that it works, and a look of its own

**Where the cohort is.** This week everyone handed the testing to their
agent, gave their devices roles, and asked for proof with every
"fixed." That is stage [04](../path/04-seeing-it-work.md), which opens on
the day I wrote to the agent that "I should not be the one testing your
work." Everyone also gave their app a look of its own, from the last
step of stage [03](../path/03-going-native.md), and opened two apps
from outside their group.

This is the week the prompt reading starts to come from the cohort
itself. Tonight's situation is a classmate's real stuck moment, shared
with their permission, which is a bigger thing to ask of someone than a
story from the path, so handle it with care.

**The agent is never the tester.**

## The opening

> Welcome back. From last week's answers, I heard [what you heard], so
> [what changes]. This week you each looked at two apps from outside
> your group, and I hope you found something in one of them you want
> to borrow. Tonight's prompt comes from one of you, with their
> permission, and I want to thank them now, before we start, because
> showing the moment you were stuck is the hardest kind of showing
> there is.

## What they bring back

The cohort's bring-back: a screenshot that proved a fix, and the app's
own look beside the template's. From the stages:

> Be ready to show the screenshot that proved a fix, to say what it
> would have looked like if the fix had not worked, and to explain which
> of your devices the agent may never touch, and why.

And from stage 03, the part that waited for this week: "why its new
look fits the people it is for."

**The bar.** For stage 04: "the agent has proven a fix to you with a
screenshot from your own device, your devices have roles, and you have
stopped being the first person to find its mistakes." For the look,
from stage 03: the app "looks like your app rather than the template."

**What to look for.**

- A screenshot taken from the student's own device, of the thing that
  was fixed, and the student's own description of what it would have
  shown if the fix had failed.
- Device roles the student can name, including the one the agent may
  never touch.
- A look that came from choosing among directions, explained by who
  the app is for rather than by what the student likes.
- The new look on every platform the app runs on, so it holds the same
  way the features do.

**Signs of "not yet."** "The agent said the tests passed," with nothing
to look at. A screenshot from a simulator standing in for the device.
An app that still wears the template's colors and type.

## The prompt to read together

**Choosing the situation.** Use a stuck moment from last week's checks
or the cohort's conversation, and ask its owner privately, before the
session, whether you may use it. Put up what they saw and what the
agent said, never what they sent, so everyone tries it fresh. Then
everyone writes their prompt alone, compares with a partner, and sees
what the classmate actually sent, along with what happened next. Let
the classmate tell the story if they would like to, and never press
them if they would rather not.

**If nobody offers one,** use this situation from stage 05. Archive
Watch was shipping a release to several platforms at once, and the
agent wrote one set of "what's new" notes for all of them. Ask what
they would send. Then show the real prompt:

> The what's new text should be different by platform because the
> features are different.

**What to talk about.** Whichever situation you use, ask the three
questions of the prompt rather than the app: what did the builder want,
what did the agent get wrong, and how did the prompt fix it.

## Start

Everyone begins [stage 05](../path/05-shipping.md). For anyone with a
store account, that means its second step, asking the agent for
everything a submission needs. For anyone without one, it means asking
for the free way onto someone else's device this week, through a test
track or a direct share. Either way, everyone sends the first prompt
before they leave, and names, to themselves, the person whose device
the app will reach.

## The check

1. **When your agent says something is fixed, what do you ask to see,
   and why is its word not enough?**
2. **What is still muddy?**

The answer you hope for names evidence from outside the agent: a
screenshot from the device, a measurement, or a test that could have
failed. An answer that trusts the agent's report is worth a word.

## The close

> Answer the two questions on the live page before you go. Next week is
> the first time your app leaves your hands. Bring it installed on a
> device you did not build it on, and bring what that person said, in
> their own words, in your note.

## After the session

Read the checks, write your two sentences, and reach out within two
days to anyone who missed the session or brought nothing back. Thank
tonight's classmate privately, and ask someone else for permission to
use their stuck moment in week 4. Week 4 is also the first of the
sessions where you look closely at every bring-back, so set aside time
the day after it.

Next week, the app on someone else's device.
