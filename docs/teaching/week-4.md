# Week 4: the app on someone else's device

**Where the cohort is.** This week everyone's app left their hands. Some
went through a store, and more went through a test track or a direct
share, which cost nothing. Everyone also started looking for one honest
number about how the app is doing out in the world. That is stages
[05](../path/05-shipping.md) and [06](../path/06-keeping-it-running.md),
whose lines are "A feature nobody can install is unfinished" and "A
confident zero is worse than no dashboard."

This is the first of the two weeks when the teacher looks closely at
every bring-back, because it is the first time the app has been in the
hands of someone who did not build it, and what that person said is
worth more than anything the builder could say about it.

**Shipped means someone else can install it.**

## The session

## The opening

> Welcome back. Last week you told me [what you heard], so [what
> changes]. This week your app reached someone who did not build it,
> and tonight I want to hear what they said, in their words. I will be
> visiting every group and staying a little longer than usual, which
> only means that this is the week the app stops being only yours.

## What they bring back

The cohort's bring-back: the app on someone else's device, and what
that person said. The stages' own lines:

> Be ready to show your app in a store, installed on a device you did
> not build it on, and to explain one thing you rewrote in its listing,
> and why.

> Be ready to show one number Pulse read correctly, one reader that told
> you honestly it could not, and what your loop finished, and to explain
> why you stopped the loop when you did.

**The bar, in the cohort's version.** The app installed on a device the
student did not build it on, through a test track, a direct share, or a
store, and one honest number about it. The stages' full bars (a store
listing a stranger could install from, Pulse reading a store, a loop
run from start to stop) often land after the cohort ends. Saying "not
yet" to those tonight is ordinary and costs nothing.

**What to look for.**

- The app running on a device that belongs to someone else, or at least
  one the student did not build it on, shown or described with that
  person's words copied into the note.
- One thing the student decided about what that person said: what they
  will change, and what they will not.
- One number with its source named, and the student able to say what it
  would look like if the source could not be read.
- If they are in a store, one thing in the listing they rewrote in their
  own words.

**Signs of "not yet."** The app only on the student's own devices. A
number nobody questioned, especially a zero. A store listing the agent
wrote that the student has not read aloud.

## The prompt to read together

**Choosing the situation.** As in week 3: a classmate's stuck moment,
used with their permission, shown as what they saw and what the agent
said, without what they sent.

**If nobody offers one,** use this situation from stage 08. On September
24, the agent working on Archive Watch needed to wake an Apple TV and
install a build, and improvised a way to do it, step by step, when a
runbook in the same repository already said exactly how and the tools
were sitting in `tools/`. Ask what they would send. Then show the real
prompt:

> You have fully documented device testing pathways for each device.
> Please stop trying to reinvent things you already know how to do.

**What to talk about.** The agent was not careless. It simply did not
remember, because every session starts fresh and the only memory it has
is what it reads. Ask what their own agent has had to be told twice,
because that is where stage 08 starts.

## Start

Everyone begins [stage 07](../path/07-raising-the-ceiling.md) the way it
begins: with a want noticed while using the app, written down in their
note in their own terms. Then they ask the agent for research before
code (whether it is possible, on which platforms, and what it would
cost), and send that before they leave.

## The check

1. **What is one thing about your app you can no longer see by using it
   yourself, and how will you find out about it?**
2. **What is still muddy?**

Good answers name something real (a crash on a device they do not own,
what strangers say about it, a store's numbers) and an honest way to
hear about it. An answer that says "I'll just keep using it" is worth a
word.

## The close

> Answer the two questions on the live page before you go. Next week is
> the last session, and it is open to anyone you would like to invite.
> Bring the feature you wanted from your own use, and one value with the
> decision it changed. And if you would like to lead the prompt
> reading next week with a moment of your own, tell me this week. Nobody
> has to.

## After the session

Read the checks, write your two sentences, and reach out within two
days to anyone who missed the session or brought nothing back. Then do
this week's close look: for each person, read what their app's first
outside user said and what they decided about it, and write them a
few sentences back. Ask anyone who has said "not yet" about the same
stage twice for fifteen minutes before week 5. Find out whether anyone
wants to lead next week's prompt reading, and help them choose their
moment. And send the invitation for the final showing, so that guests
can come.

Next week, everyone's work, shown to everyone.
