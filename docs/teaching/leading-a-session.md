# Leading a session

On November 18, 2026, I read the plan for a cohort's third session and
found big slides that said things like "Your agent's word is not proof,"
and a "question for everyone" that was really an instruction to write a
prompt. None of it was about the apps in the room. We are together in a
session to share our work and to develop what comes next, and a plan
that is invented fresh every week gives nobody a way to make progress.

So every session runs the same routine, built on the way I build apps.
I use the app, I write down what I saw as a numbered list, I hand that
list to the agent one item at a time, and I check each fix on a real
device before the next round. A session is that round, done with other
people's hands on the app.

**The routine stays the same; the apps and the questions change.**

## What happens before

Each person answers one question on the session page: what they want
someone to try, and the one thing they want to know about it. That is
the builder's own question, about something they can change themselves,
and it leads their turn ([research](../research/synchronous-sessions.md),
the Tuning Protocol and the Consultancy protocol).

The leader reads every answer, opens every app at its address, and looks
at each repository's history, branches included, because "what changed
this week" is a fact in the commits, not a guess. Then the leader writes
the plan's slides from what they found: each person's name, their plan
from last week in their own words, and what their app does today. A
slide that could be shown to any cohort is a slide that is not ready.

## The four parts

### 1. Your plans from last week (about 6 minutes)

The stage shows a table: each person, the plan they named at the end of
the last session, and what their app does today. In a fixed order, each
person says two lines, "My plan was ___" and "What happened was ___",
in about thirty seconds. "Not started yet" is an ordinary answer, said
plainly, and the leader moves on. When someone changed their plan, the
leader asks what changed it, because that is usually the most useful
thing anyone says in the hour.

This is where last week's plan is checked, out loud, which is the part
of a public commitment the evidence supports. The plan and its result
go public; nobody is praised for an intention.

### 2. Try each app (about 9 minutes each)

Every builder gets the same nine minutes, in the same order:

1. **The builder asks their one question** (1 minute), from what they
   wrote before the session.
2. **Everyone tries the app, quietly** (3 minutes), in their own copy
   on the stage, with Show my app to everyone.
3. **Each person says what they did and what happened** (2 minutes),
   with "I tried to ___," "What happened was ___," and "I expected
   ___." Nobody says what they think of the app until they have said
   what they did.
4. **One "I wonder if"** from anyone (1 minute).
5. **The builder explains one decision, with the agent closed, and
   what they will change first** (2 minutes). A decision, never the
   code: if they cannot explain it, that is their next prompt
   ([Showing your work](../path/showing-your-work.md)).

The builder listens and writes; they do not explain or defend while
people are saying what happened. The leader calls on people by name, in
the same order each time, after the three minutes, and waits after each
answer.

A builder with nothing new to try still gets their nine minutes: they
say who the app is for and what its first screen would show, each person
asks one question to understand it, and they leave with the first prompt
half written.

### 3. Write your next round (about 13 minutes)

The stage shows a round I actually sent to an agent, so that people see
the shape before they write: each line is something I saw while using
the app, then what I wanted instead. Then the form:

- I tried my app, and here is what I saw: 1. ___ 2. ___ 3. ___
- Start with ___
- This is done when ___
- It must never ___

Everyone writes in the box in their panel and sends the same words to
their agent before the part ends, so the week has already begun when
they leave. "This is done when" is what they will see on their own
screen. "It must never" comes from their app's own rules. In the last
two minutes each person reads their "Start with" line aloud, in the same
order, with no applause and no comment. Those lines open the next
session.

### 4. Your questions (about 3 minutes)

One private question in the panel, always the same: "What questions do
you still have?" The leader answers two or three aloud and the rest
before the next session, and opens that session by saying what they
read.

## Words on the stage and in the panel

Every word we put on the main stage or in the panel has to mean
something to someone who has never seen it before.

1. **A question is a question.** If it tells people to do something,
   it is a task, and it reads as one: "Write your next round," with the
   form, never "A question for everyone: write your next prompt."
2. **Ask what someone did or saw before what it means.** "What did you
   do first when it opened?" comes before "What should change?"
3. **The builder's question leads.** The leader does not write one
   question for every app.
4. **One ask at a time, with time to write and named turns.**
5. **Name the real thing.** A slide names the person, their app, their
   plan, and their words. "Your agent's word is not proof" names
   nothing anyone in the room did, so it does not go on a slide. The
   same idea, said about one builder's pathway explorer and what they saw on
   his own screen, does.
6. **Button names say what pressing them does.** Show my app to
   everyone, Next part, Close it. A tab or heading that names nothing
   (it was "Mine") comes out.

The sources for each rule are in
[`docs/research/synchronous-sessions.md`](../research/synchronous-sessions.md):
Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process, the Right Question Institute,
the School Reform Initiative's protocols, Liberating Structures, Mary
Budd Rowe on wait time, and the research on progress monitoring and
public intentions.

## What the leader does in the add-on

- **Before people arrive:** open the activity, then Open the main stage.
  The welcome shows the session's title.
- **To begin:** Start the session. Next part moves everyone to the next
  part; within a part, Next slide walks its slides. Coming next, at the
  foot of Now, names the part after this one.
- **During Try each app:** each builder presses Show my app to everyone
  in their panel, and the leader presses Show it under Who is ready to
  show. A screen share is the fallback when the version to try is not
  at the builder's address yet.
- **During a question or Write your next round:** the answers arrive in
  Read the answers. Close it ends a question for everyone, and the stage
  says it is closed.
- **At the end:** End activity in Meet, after the last part's answers
  are in.

## What this costs

Running the same routine every week trades surprise for progress. Some
weeks a new idea from the method deserves twenty minutes of the hour,
and this routine gives it none. When that happens, the idea goes on one
slide inside Write your next round, said about someone's real app, or
it waits for a week when fewer people have something to try. The cost
of saying what everyone did out loud is real too: nobody can hide a
quiet week. That is why "not started yet" is said plainly and passed
over, and why the next round is written in the room.

Bring one app, one question, and last week's plan.
