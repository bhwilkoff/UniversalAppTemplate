# Week 5: a feature you wanted, shown to everyone

**Where the cohort is.** Week 4 was [stage 06](../path/06-keeping-it-running.md)
and [stage 07](../path/07-raising-the-ceiling.md). Everyone read one
honest number about their app and took it back to a person, kept a log
while using the app, and built a feature they wanted from their own
use, with a rough version in front of someone it is for.

Tonight shows that work, and starts the last stage:
[stage 08](../path/08-working-with-ai.md), written in the days after the
showing, where the agent learns to remember what this app taught, and
the builder names one value with the decision it changed, because a
value that never changed a decision is decoration.

Tonight is the final showing, open to everyone, guests welcome. It is
also the second week the teacher looks closely at every bring-back,
and the night the cohort chooses the one lesson it sends back to the
template. The session keeps its four parts: the showing itself is the
bring-back of stages 06 and 07, a short talk on how values climb from
reason to rule to check is the one thing that unblocks stage 08, Start
is the cohort's lesson and everyone's first prompt of stage 08, and the
check closes the night.

**Everyone's work, shown to everyone.**

## The move and the question

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (rebuilt by the method of
curriculum research note 06, October 7, 2026).*

**Publish.** This week publishes the app to everyone, guests welcome,
and the writing that follows (stage 08) keeps what the builder learned
for the agent and the next builder.

The question the whole session turns on, in plain words: **Is this
version enough for its people, and what should the next builder not
have to learn?**

The session is designed backward
([note 06](../research/curriculum/06-instructional-design.md), section 4)
from the showing's bring-back (the honest number with what its people
said, the feature from the student's own use, and two sentences on what
is enough, for now, and for whom) and from what stage 08 asks for in
the days after (one value with the decision it changed, and
`HUMAN-SHAPED.md` in the student's own words). For stage 08, Ben's own
material is the model students reason with: the night he refused a paid voice server for
Archive Watch because his apps cost $0 to run, from the September
section of the [Archive Watch case study](../human-shaped/case-study-archive-watch.md),
which ends with values climbing "from a reason, to a standing
instruction, to a test." It is a model of what a value tied to a decision
looks like, not an answer to copy. A public product, shown to people
beyond the cohort, with a reflection on it, is how a project ends
([PBLWorks, Gold Standard Project Design Elements](https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design)).

Every session is one round of Write, Play, Publish at a larger size, the
same three moves as stage 00, and the principles decide what gets
written, played with, and published.

## The opening

> Welcome, everyone, and a special welcome to our guests. Tonight is the
> end of this cohort, and the start of whatever each of these apps does
> next. Before we begin, the four rules we have used all along, for our
> guests' sake: no correcting what does not matter, no acting surprised,
> no taking over, and no small digs at who someone is. From last week's
> answers, I heard [what you heard]. Groups first, one last time, and
> then everyone shows everyone.

In the 60-minute session, where everyone stays in one call, leave out
"Groups first, one last time," because the showing follows Arrive
directly.

## What they bring back

The cohort's bring-back: the honest number with what its people said,
and the feature. The stages' own lines:

> Be ready to show one number Pulse read correctly, one reader that told
> you honestly it could not, and what your loop finished, and to explain
> why you stopped the loop when you did.

> Be ready to show the feature on your newest device, the sentence an
> older device shows instead, and one thing someone else changed about
> it, and to explain why you decided it should exist at all.

**The bar.** For stage 06: Pulse "reads at least one place the app lives
honestly," and "one honest number has gone back to a person, with what
they said in your note." For stage 07: "a feature you wanted from your
own use is live on every platform that can have it, someone other than
you has shaped it, and the older devices say honestly what they cannot
do." Stage 08 follows the showing, and its bar is met in the days after.

**What to look for.**

- One number with its source named, the student able to say what it
  would look like if the source could not be read, and what the person
  they showed it to said it misses.
- A feature the student can trace back to a want they wrote down while
  using the app.
- One thing someone else changed about it, and what the student gave
  back to them.

**Signs of "not yet."** A number nobody questioned, especially a zero.
A feature nobody but the builder has touched. Many apps will be "not yet"
on some part of these stages tonight, and that is ordinary. The stages are
built to be lapped more than once, and the app's page stays open.

## Last look in your group

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (the rewrite of October 23, 2026).*

Send everyone to their group's room for one last look before the
showing. Each builder gets about four minutes: three to show the feature
and the number once, on the device, the way they will show them to
everyone, and one to explain a part of the app the agent wrote and how
they know it works. That last minute is stage 08's honesty in the log at
the size of one feature, because "the agent said it works" is fixed,
and something outside the agent seeing it work is verified. Visit the
rooms, say so on arriving so that nobody stops to perform, and listen
for a part its builder cannot explain. That is not a failure tonight,
only the next prompt, and [Showing your work](../path/showing-your-work.md)
has one: "Before we change anything else, explain why we decided to do
it this way, in plain words, and tell me what we gave up."

In the 60-minute session there are no rooms, and the rehearsal happens
in the showing itself.

## The showing

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (the rewrite of October 23, 2026).*

This is the bring-back of stages 06 and 07, shown to the whole room,
guests included. Each builder has about three minutes to show the
feature they wanted from living with their app on their newest device,
the sentence an older device shows instead, and their one honest number
with what its people said. Keep time gently, and after each builder let
the guests ask one question each, nudging them toward the kind
[Showing your work](../path/showing-your-work.md) recommends, a question
about something the agent could not have decided, such as "What did you
give up?" or "Who is this for?" This is one of the two nights the
teacher looks closely at every bring-back, so note against the stage
bars above who is ready and who is "not yet," without saying it out
loud.

Watch for anyone reading their agent's words instead of their own, and
ask them what they decided. Watch, too, for a zero nobody questioned,
and for an older device that fails silently rather than saying what it
cannot do. In the 60-minute session the showing follows Arrive
directly.

## From reason to rule to check

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (the rewrite of October 23, 2026).*

A six-minute talk, and the one thing that unblocks stage 08. Walk the
ladder from [stage 08](../path/08-working-with-ai.md): a reason is the
"Why we build" paragraph the agent weighs like advice; a rule is a
standing instruction in the builder's own words, dated the day the agent
crossed a line, which the agent reads every session but can still miss;
and a check is a test or a hook that fails when the line is crossed, so
the agent cannot cross it without being told. Not every value can climb
all the way, and "only essential words on screen" will probably always
need a person's judgment, but "one spelling locale" climbed from a word
Ben noticed on a label to a test that reads every string in the app.

For a value tied to a decision, use the September section of the
[Archive Watch case study](../human-shaped/case-study-archive-watch.md):
on September 20 the agent designed Watch Together around a paid voice
server, and Ben refused, because "the goal of this app (and all of my
apps) is for them to cost $0 to run." In the same week, three standing
instructions went into the app's `CLAUDE.md`. It is a model of what
a value that changed a decision looks like, not an answer to copy. Then
point each builder at their own app: they have just shown a feature,
and somewhere in building it a value of theirs either changed a
decision or did not, and stage 08 is where they find out which, and
where the lessons that did climb get written down so the next app starts
knowing them.

## Start

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (the rewrite of October 23, 2026).*

First, the cohort chooses its one lesson to send back to the template,
as [`COURSE.md`](../../COURSE.md) describes in "What a cohort gives
back": a prompt that worked, a step that broke, or a correction that
kept coming up, drawn from the apps just shown. Offer two or three
candidates you saw in the weeks' checks, let the cohort choose, and ask
who would like to be credited. It is one lesson from the whole cohort,
never a requirement for any one person.

Then everyone sends the first prompt of
[stage 08](../path/08-working-with-ai.md) before they leave. The stage
asks for a first answer before any prompt, so give a minute for each
builder to write in their note the one lesson from their own app they
would most want carried forward, and then to ask their agent which of
their app's lessons belong in a skill or a memory. Keep a room open for
anyone who gets stuck. The rest of stage 08 is written in the days after
the showing, and whatever comes next for the app (the next round, a
store listing, or the first stage of a second lap) waits until stage 08
has kept what this one taught. In the 60-minute session, Start has two
more minutes, which is enough for the cohort to choose without rushing.

## The check

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (the rewrite of October 23, 2026).*

The two question scenes at the end of the run of show, answered
privately, and shown on the main stage without names only if you
choose:

1. **Enough, for now:** "What is enough about this version, for now,
   and for whom?" Principle 15 is the measure: an app that "brings joy
   to at least one person in the form it ships today" is enough, for
   now. Read for whether the "whom" is the people the builder talked to
   in week 1.
2. **What is still muddy?** (in their own words).

**Also prepared,** in the cohort's question bank: "What waits for the
next version?" and "What will your agent know at the start of your next
app that it did not know in week 1, and where does that knowledge live?"

There is no next session to open with what you heard, so this week the
teacher writes it to the cohort's conversation instead.

## The task, and its evidence

*Written by Claude, awaiting Ben's review (curriculum milestone C5, October 7, 2026).*

**The task.** Show the honest number and the feature to everyone. Decide what is enough, for now, and for whom. Then, in the days after the showing, write stage 08: a skill and a handoff, and `HUMAN-SHAPED.md`, with one value tied to the decision it changed.

**Evidence a person can open.** The number and what its people said; the feature and the log entry it came from; one thing someone else changed about it; the two sentences on what is enough; the list of what waits; and, after the showing, the skill and the declaration.

**On the credential.** Recognitions: "Shared what they learned," "Credited the people and tools behind it," "Found whose work they are building on." Students do the thing itself, and what they
make is the evidence; nobody writes a report about it
([`05-inquiry-and-evidence.md`](../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md)).

## When someone is stuck

Every entry in the [stuck library](../stuck/README.md) starts with a
question to ask yourself, then what to try, then who to ask. This week,
point people to:

- [Stuck writing](../stuck/writing/README.md), for when the problem, the why, or what comes next will not come.
- [Stuck publishing](../stuck/publishing/README.md), for when you are not ready to show anyone, cannot get it onto another device, or do not know when it is enough.

And the ladder for any of them: name the kind of stuck, write three
lines (what you tried, what you expected, what you saw), ask the agent
for a way to understand it rather than the fix, look on the device and
at the last thing that worked, then your trio, then the teacher. About
twenty minutes before the trio is a default each person can move.

> read every one and write back to all of you. Thank you for showing
> unfinished work to one another for five weeks. Nobody graduates out of
> this community, and your app's page stays open while it keeps
> growing. What will you build next?

## After the session

Read the checks, and post what you heard to the cohort's conversation,
with what you would change for the next cohort. Send the cohort's
lesson back to the template as an issue or a pull request, with the
people who learned it credited. Write, privately, to anyone who left
with a "not yet," to say that the stages and the community stay open.
Then tell me what broke, as an issue on the template, so the next
teacher does not have to learn it again.

That is a cohort, start to finish.
