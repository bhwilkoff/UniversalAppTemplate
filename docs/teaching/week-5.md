**Where the cohort is.** Week 4 was [stage 06](../path/06-keeping-it-running.md)
and [stage 07](../path/07-raising-the-ceiling.md). Everyone read one
honest number about their app and took it back to a person, kept a log
while using the app, and built a feature they wanted from their own
use, with a rough version in front of someone it is for.

Tonight is the final showing, open to everyone, guests welcome, and it
is the second week the teacher looks closely at every build. It ends by
starting the last stage, [stage 08](../path/08-working-with-ai.md),
written in the days after, where the agent learns to remember what this
app taught and the builder names one value with the decision it
changed.

**Everyone's work, shown to everyone.**

## The move and the question

**Publish.** This week publishes the app to everyone, guests welcome,
and the writing that follows (stage 08) keeps what the builder learned
for the agent and the next builder. A public product, shown to people
beyond the cohort, with a reflection on it, is how a project ends
([PBLWorks, Gold Standard Project Design Elements](https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design)).

The question the week turns on: **Is this version enough for its
people, and what should the next builder not have to learn?**

**What to watch for tonight.**

- A part of the app its builder cannot explain, even one the agent
  wrote.
- A number nobody questioned, especially a zero.
- Anyone reading their agent's words at the showing instead of their
  own. Ask them what they decided.
- Whether each builder can name what is enough, for now, for the people
  they talked to in week 1.

## What they show

Before the session, read each person's answer on the session page, open
every app at its address, and write a slide for each builder with their
name, their app, and the feature they wanted. The stages ask each
builder to be ready for this:

> Be ready to show one number Pulse read correctly, one reader that told
> you honestly it could not, and what your loop finished, and to explain
> why you stopped the loop when you did.

> Be ready to show the feature on your newest device, the sentence an
> older device shows instead, and one thing someone else changed about
> it, and to explain why you decided it should exist at all.

**The bar.** For stage 06: Pulse "reads at least one place the app lives
honestly," and "one honest number has gone back to a person, with what
they said in your note." For stage 07: "a feature you wanted from your
own use is live on every platform that can have it, someone other than
you has shaped it, and the older devices say honestly what they cannot
do." Many apps will be "not yet" on some part of these tonight, and
that is ordinary, because the stages are built to be lapped more than
once and the app's page stays open.

## Welcome

Say who is here, and that anyone, guests included, can try each app in
their own copy on the stage. Something like this:

> Welcome, everyone, and a special welcome to our guests. Tonight is the
> end of this cohort, and the start of whatever each of these apps does
> next. For our guests' sake, here are the four rules we have used all
> along: no correcting what does not matter, no acting surprised, no
> taking over, and no small digs at who someone is. From last week's
> answers, I heard [what you heard]. Each builder will show their app,
> you will try it, and they will tell you one decision behind it.

## The showing

Share the time evenly among the builders, in a fixed order, and keep
the routine the cohort has used all along. The builder says who the app
is for, everyone tries it in their own copy, guests included, and then
each person says what they did and what happened before anyone says
what they think. The builder ends by explaining one decision, with the
agent closed, and what waits for the next version. Guests often reach
for praise or advice, so ask them first for what they tried, and let
them ask a question about something the agent could not have decided,
such as "What did you give up?" or "Who is this for?"
([Showing your work](../path/showing-your-work.md)).

The decision is where stage 08 begins, so listen for a value behind it.
A builder who says "I refused that because..." has a value that changed
a decision, and that is worth naming back to them in a sentence. Listen,
too, for a part its builder cannot explain, which is not a failure
tonight but the next prompt, and Showing your work has one: "Before we
change anything else, explain why we decided to do it this way, in
plain words, and tell me what we gave up." Watch for a zero nobody
questioned, and for an older device that fails silently rather than
saying what it cannot do. Note against the bars above who is ready and
who is "not yet," without saying it out loud.

## What the next builder should not have to learn

Show a real lesson first, so that people see the shape before they
write. The one I use is from the September section of the [Archive Watch case
study](../human-shaped/case-study-archive-watch.md): on September 20 the
agent designed Watch Together around a paid voice server, and I refused,
because "the goal of this app (and all of my apps) is for them to cost
$0 to run." In the same week, three standing instructions went into the
app's `CLAUDE.md`, so the next session started knowing it. Then the
form:

- The lesson: ___
- Where it lives now (a memory, a skill, a decision): ___

The second line is the ladder from stage 08, said about each person's
own lesson. A reason in the "Why we build" paragraph is weighed like
advice; a rule, dated and in the builder's words, is read every session
but can still be missed; and a check is a test or a hook that fails when
the line is crossed. "One spelling locale" climbed from a word I noticed
on a label to a test that reads every string in the app, while "only
essential words on screen" will probably always need a person's
judgment, so not every lesson climbs all the way, and a lesson with
nowhere to live yet is the first prompt of stage 08.

Everyone writes in the box in their panel and sends the same words to
their agent before the part ends, asking it where that lesson belongs.
In the last two minutes each person reads their lesson aloud, in the
same order, with no applause and no comment. From those lines the
cohort chooses one to send back to the template, as
[`COURSE.md`](../../COURSE.md) describes in "What a cohort gives back":
let the cohort choose, and ask who would like to be credited. It is one
lesson from the whole cohort, never a requirement for any one person.

## Your questions

One private question in the panel: "What questions do you still have?"
Only the leaders read the answers. There is no next session to open
with what you read, so this week you write it to the cohort's
conversation instead, answering every question there.

The week's own question, "What is enough about this version, for now,
and for whom?", is best asked on the session page before the showing,
beside each builder's own question. Principle 15 is the measure: an app
that "brings joy to at least one person in the form it ships today" is
enough, for now. Read for whether the "whom" is the people the builder
talked to in week 1. "What waits for the next version?" and "What will
your agent know at the start of your next app that it did not know in
week 1, and where does that knowledge live?" stay in the question bank.

## The task, and its evidence

**The task.** Show the honest number and the feature to everyone.
Decide what is enough, for now, and for whom. Then, in the days after
the showing, write stage 08: a skill and a handoff, and
`HUMAN-SHAPED.md`, with one value tied to the decision it changed.

**Evidence a person can open.** The number and what its people said;
the feature and the log entry it came from; one thing someone else
changed about it; the two sentences on what is enough; the list of what
waits; and, after the showing, the skill and the declaration.

**On the credential.** Recognitions: "Shared what they learned,"
"Credited the people and tools behind it," "Found whose work they are
building on." Students do the thing itself, and what they make is the
evidence; nobody writes a report about it
([`05-inquiry-and-evidence.md`](../research/curriculum/05-inquiry-and-evidence.md)).

## When someone is stuck

Every entry in the [stuck library](../stuck/README.md) starts with a
question to ask yourself, then what to try, then who to ask. This week,
point people to:

- [Stuck writing](../stuck/writing/README.md), for when the problem, the why, or what comes next will not come.
- [Stuck publishing](../stuck/publishing/README.md), for when you are not ready to show anyone, cannot get it onto another device, or do not know when it is enough.

And the ladder for any of them: name the kind of stuck, write three
lines (what you tried, what you expected, what you saw), ask the agent
for a way to understand it rather than the fix, look on the device and
at the last thing that worked, then your group, then the teacher. About
twenty minutes before the group is a default each person can move.

## The close

> Answer the question in your panel before you go, and I will read
> every one and write back to all of you. Thank you for showing
> unfinished work to one another for five weeks. Nobody graduates out of
> this community, and your app's page stays open while it keeps
> growing. What will you build next?

## After the session

Read the answers, and post what you heard to the cohort's conversation,
with what you would change for the next cohort. Send the cohort's
lesson back to the template as an issue or a pull request, with the
people who learned it credited. Write, privately, to anyone who left
with a "not yet," to say that the stages and the community stay open.
Then tell me what broke, as an issue on the template, so the next
teacher does not have to learn it again.

That is a cohort, start to finish.
