2. What share of nights are shorter than 6 hours?

Students' nightly sleep follows a normal curve: mean 7.1 hours, SD 0.9 hours.

pnorm(x, mean, sd) returns the area to the left of x: the share below x. A z-score, (x − mean) / SD, says how many SDs x is from the mean; pnorm(z) gives the same area on the standard normal curve (mean 0, SD 1).

Find the z-score of 6 hours as z_short and the share of nights under 6 hours as p_short. Then move the slider to 6 to check them.

Hint 1A z-score counts SDs from the mean: subtract the mean, then divide by the SD. mu and sigma are already set.
Hint 2z_short <- (6 - mu) / sigma. For p_short, copy the worked example's line with 6 in place of 8; pnorm(z_short) should print the same number.

3. How do you go back from a share to hours?

Sleep: mean 7.1 hours, SD 0.9 hours (mu and sigma).

A night at the 90th percentile is longer than 90% of nights. qnorm(share, mean, sd) runs pnorm backwards: it takes a share and returns the value with that share to its left. Without mean and sd, qnorm(share) returns the z-score.

In your own words

What goes into pnorm, and what comes out? And qnorm? One or two sentences.

One way to say it: pnorm takes a value (or a z-score) and returns the area to its left, a share between 0 and 1. qnorm does the reverse: it takes a share and returns the value with that share to its left. qnorm(pnorm(6, 7.1, 0.9), 7.1, 0.9) gives back 6.

Compare: did yours say which side the area is on (to the left)? A share of nights longer than 9 hours needs 1 - pnorm(9, mu, sigma).

4. How many of 16 admits will say yes?

Suppose Minerva's yield is 70%: 70% of admitted students accept their offer. You meet a random 16 recently admitted students; each accepts with probability 0.7, independently.

The number who accept is binomial: n = 16, p = 0.7. Its mean is n × p; its SD is √(n × p × (1 − p)) = 1.83. dbinom(k, size, prob) is the height of bar k; pbinom(k, size, prob) adds the bars from 0 up to k, including k.

Compute expected (how many of the 16 you expect to accept), p_14_16 (14, 15 or 16 accept) and p_under13 (fewer than 13 accept).

Hint 1A binomial's mean is trials × chance. For 14 to 16, add three bars: dbinom accepts 14:16 as k.
Hint 2expected <- n * p and p_14_16 <- sum(dbinom(14:16, size = n, prob = p)). For p_under13: which is the largest count that is fewer than 13? Give that k to pbinom(k, size = n, prob = p).
