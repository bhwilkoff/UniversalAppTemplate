Which effect, which assumptions

Assumes you have read Ch. 5 of Cunningham's Causal Inference: The Remix (§5.3). Work through the numbered steps in order. About 25 minutes.

Haven't read it? Here's what you need

The ATE, ATT and ATU average a treatment's effect over everyone, over the treated, or over the untreated. To estimate one, compare treated and untreated people inside each group (here, age groups), then average those differences with the right weights.

That works only under two assumptions. Unconfoundedness: inside each group, who is treated is as good as random. Common support: each group has both treated and untreated people to compare.

Assumptions behind the numbers
  • Parameter. 3,000 people, 1,000 per age group. Without the vaccine 90%, 80% and 60% of the young, middle-aged and old stay healthy. The vaccine adds 2, 6 and 15 points.
  • Parameter. The shares vaccinated move in steps of 5% and start at 20%, 50% and 80%. Steps 3 to 7 reset them when you enter.
  • Simplification. Every number is a population mean, so there is no sampling noise.
  • Simplification. Under the coin flip, vaccination within an age group is unrelated to Y0 and Y1, so unconfoundedness holds by construction.
  • Distribution. Hidden frailty: within each group the vaccinated have Y0 6 points below the unvaccinated. Each group's mean Y0 and effect stay the same.
  • Distribution. Selection on gains: within each group the vaccinated gain 6 points more than the unvaccinated would. Y0 stays balanced, and each group's mean effect stays the same.
  • Interpretation. An estimate shows n/a when a group it needs lacks vaccinated or unvaccinated people. The true value still exists; the data just can't reach it.

Left out: the weights a regression with an age dummy puts on each group, which the page “Which effect does the regression estimate?” covers (it says ATC for the ATU); sampling noise; and many covariates at once.
