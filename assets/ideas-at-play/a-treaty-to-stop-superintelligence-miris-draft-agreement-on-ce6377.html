A treaty to stop superintelligence

In November 2025 the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI) published a draft international agreement to halt the development of artificial superintelligence: AI far more capable than humans at nearly everything. It starts as a deal between the United States and China that other countries can join. Its lever is compute, the arithmetic it takes to train an AI model, measured in FLOP (floating-point operations).

Cap the training runs

Any training run above 1024 FLOP is banned; fine-tuning an existing model (post-training) above 1023 is banned; runs above 1022 are reported and monitored.

Control the chips

Any cluster of 16 or more H100-equivalent AI chips (about $500,000 of hardware) must sit in a monitored facility. Chip supply chains and transfers are tracked.

Restrict the research

Research that makes training cheaper or AI chips faster, or pushes general AI toward superintelligence, is controlled or banned. Narrow uses (medicine, science, industry) are allowed.

Who enforces it?

  • An Executive Council, at first just the US and China, deciding by unanimity, and a Coalition Technical Body that tracks chips, inspects facilities (including challenge inspections at short notice), evaluates models and can update the thresholds.
  • Verification through monitored facilities, chip tracking, whistleblower channels, and each superpower’s own intelligence services.
  • Against violators (Article XII): trade and asset restrictions, visa bans, appeals to the UN Security Council and, in the extreme, targeted cyber or military action.
  • Leaving takes 12 months’ notice, during which the others may act to stop the leaver keeping the capacity to build superintelligence. The agreement has no end date; a review conference meets every three years.

Debates to have: Would the US and China sign while each fears the other is ahead? Can hidden clusters, smuggled chips or training spread across many small sites be caught? Are the research limits enforceable, or the right price? What does a halt that may last decades cost in AI benefits forgone?
