Causal inference · Unconfoundedness · Remix §5.1

Same estimator, four ways to assign treatment

Assumes you have read Ch. 5 of Cunningham's Causal Inference: The Remix (§5.1). Work through the numbered steps in order. About 25 minutes.

Haven't read it? Here's what you need

Each student has two potential outcomes: a test score with tutoring (Y¹) and one without (Y⁰). You only ever see one of them. The ATE is the average gain from tutoring over all students. The ATT and ATU are the same average, taken only over the tutored or only over the untutored.

The SDO is the raw comparison: the tutored group's average score minus everyone else's. It equals the ATE only when who joins has nothing to do with those potential outcomes. This page tries four rules for who joins.

Read the four rules, then predict and lock in.

  • Weighted coin: 0.75 for Low, 0.25 for High.
  • Struggling sign up: students who would score low untutored join more often.
  • Big gainers sign up: in each group, those who would gain more join more often.

What is the difference in means made of?

Read the three parts, then answer.

SDO = ATE + E[Y⁰ | D = 1] − E[Y⁰ | D = 0] + (1 − π)(ATT − ATU)

  • ATE: the average gain δ = Y¹ − Y⁰ over everyone.
  • Selection bias: the groups' gap if no one were tutored. (E[·] is an average; D = 1 means tutored.)
  • HTE bias: ATT − ATU, weighted by 1 − π, the share untreated.

Assumptions behind the numbers
  • Data. Simulated, not real: 400 students, 200 with Low and 200 with High prior achievement. The outcome is an end-of-year test score.
  • Distribution. Untreated score Y⁰ = 62 (Low) or 76 (High), plus Normal noise with SD 7. Gain δ = 10 (Low) or 4 (High), plus independent Normal noise with SD 3. ATE = 7.03.
  • Parameter. Probability of joining: coin flip 0.5; weighted coin 0.75 (Low) and 0.25 (High); struggling sign up 1 / (1 + e(Y⁰ − 70)/4); big gainers 1 / (1 + e−(δ − group mean δ)).
  • Simplification. The 400 students are the whole population. A re-run redraws only who joins, one draw per student, so the ATE never changes. Everyone complies, with no spillovers.
  • Interpretation. Selection bias, HTE bias, ATT and ATU are averages over the current draw, so the identity holds exactly. Averages over 200 re-runs stand in for expectations.

Left out: regression, matching and common support (§5.3–5.4), and how sample size changes the spread of the estimates.
