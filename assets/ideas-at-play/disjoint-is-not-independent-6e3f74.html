2. Disjoint, independent, or neither?

Disjoint: A and B can't happen together, so P(A and B) = 0.

Independent: knowing A happened doesn't change the chance of B, so P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

Can two events, each with a chance above 0, be both disjoint and independent?

Yes, often: disjoint events don't affect each other
No, never
Sometimes, depending on the events

Try pairs with the menus. Disjoint events give P(A and B) = 0, but P(A) × P(B) is above 0, so they can never be independent. Disjoint events are strongly dependent: once A happens, B can't. Use the addition rule P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) for disjoint events; use the multiplication rule P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) only for independent ones.

5. Your own events

As a group: pick two events from your own life or studies (rain tomorrow and your bus running late, say, but find your own). Are they disjoint, independent or neither, and what would you check to decide before you add or multiply their chances?

Points to compare
  • Disjoint is about overlap: can both happen on the same occasion? One shared outcome settles it, like the AB squares in step 1.
  • Independent is about information: does learning A change the chance of B? Real pairs like rain and a late bus usually fail this, so multiplying their chances is wrong.
  • Two events with chances above 0 can't be both disjoint and independent: if they are disjoint, learning A tells you B didn't happen (step 2).
  • Add for “or”, taking out the overlap unless the events are disjoint; multiply for “and” only after checking independence (steps 2 and 4).

You should now be able to:

  • tell whether two events are disjoint by looking for an outcome in both (step 1);
  • explain why disjoint events with chances above 0 are never independent (step 2), and find a pair that overlaps yet is independent (step 3);
  • choose the addition or the multiplication rule, and take out the overlap when there is one (step 4).

Assumptions
  • distributionTwo fair dice, rolled independently: all 36 ordered outcomes (first die, second die) are equally likely, each 1/36.
  • interpretation“A 3 shows” means at least one die shows a 3 (11 outcomes). Probabilities are exact counts out of 36, so independence is checked exactly: 36 × count(A and B) = count(A) × count(B).
  • interpretationDisjoint: no outcome in both. Independent: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Events with probability 0 are not in the menu.
