The two lenses

One reasonable perspective on the question of whether an argument is deductive or inductive is the view that an argument simply is, and that deduction and induction are lenses we choose to look through. Argument H is the test case. Change the lens, change what you are allowed to ask.

Argument H

The sentence never changes. The highlighted phrase carries two readings, and which one you take decides what the argument claims.

All mammalian species produce milk for their young. Therefore, the next mammal discovered will produce milk for its young.

The three interpretations

Wilkins gives Argument H three readings. Each cell below is one of them.

Premise as empirical summaryPremise as definition
Inductive lens Interpretation 1. Strong induction — a generalization from observed mammals, projected onto one not yet found. Not applicable.
Deductive lens Interpretation 2. Invalid deduction — the premises can be true while the conclusion is false. Interpretation 3. Valid deduction — a milkless creature would simply not be a mammal.

Factor 3 in the reading says deduction belongs to rule-based systems and induction to observation. Notice that the definitional reading does not just change the verdict — it changes which lens the argument invites.
