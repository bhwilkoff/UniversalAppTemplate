3Where do | and xor disagree?

Compare inclusive | with xor over the whole table. Agree with your group on which row it is.

  1. In the cell, replace NA (the first word after n_diff <-) with a calculation that counts the rows where (P | Q) and xor(P, Q) give different values. Use the columns tt$P and tt$Q. Leave the other two lines as they are.
  2. Run the cell. n_diff should be a single number. The table above it shows both columns side by side.
Hintsum() of a TRUE/FALSE vector counts the TRUEs. The comparison a != b is TRUE where they differ.

Exclusive or (⊕) is TRUE exactly when P and Q have different truth values: P ⊕ Q = (P ∨ Q) ∧ ¬(P ∧ Q).

Inclusive | and xor differ in one row only, the row where both are TRUE. R’s | is always inclusive.

  • Simplification: P and Q are atomic, two-valued sentences, with no NA.
