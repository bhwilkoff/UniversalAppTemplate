Did the Queenpin Reduce Violence?

Investigating a 2026 synthetic-control study of a woman cartel leader.

Cartel-related homicide rate, Tijuana Cartel vs “synthetic Tijuana”

What the synthetic Tijuana is made of

1. What does the study claim?

The case. The Tijuana Cartel, one of Mexico's largest drug-trafficking organizations, was run by the Arellano Félix siblings. In October 2008 the last brother in charge was arrested, and their sister, Enedina Arellano Félix, took over. She had worked in the cartel's finances, not its violent wing. She is one of very few women ever to lead a major cartel.

The question. Did the cartel's violence change under her? There is no second Tijuana Cartel without Enedina to compare with, so the study builds one.

The method: synthetic control. Take Mexico's seven other major cartels. Find the weights on them whose blend best matches Tijuana's homicide rate (and a few other traits) in 2000–2008, before she took over. After 2008, that blend, the “synthetic Tijuana”, stands in for a Tijuana Cartel that kept a male leader.

The claim. Over 2009–2018 the Tijuana Cartel's homicide rate averaged 13.5, against 17.97 for the synthetic: about 4.5 lower. The authors read this, with similar results for other outcomes, as women leaders using less visible violence.

Quintanilla Domínguez and Alcocer (2026), “Queenpins: Women Leaders and Violence in Organized Crime,” Comparative Political Studies. Rates are homicides in the municipalities where each cartel operates. Every number in this page comes from re-running the authors' published data and code.

2. Does Tijuana stand out?

A placebo test repeats the whole analysis seven more times, pretending each other cartel got a new leader in 2008. If Tijuana's gap is real, it should be bigger than the gaps that appear where nothing happened.

The test ranks the eight cartels by how much their gap grows after 2008 compared with before. The p-value is Tijuana's rank divided by 8. Tijuana ranks first, so p = 1/8.

The study looks at seven outcomes (homicides, disappearances, threatening public messages, political assassinations and more), all with this test. At the usual 5% significance level, how many of the seven can ever be significant?

3. Does the result depend on one cartel?

Half of the synthetic Tijuana (0.51) is the Juárez Cartel. In 2008–2012 Juárez fought a war for Ciudad Juárez, which became one of the most violent cities in the world.

Drop Juárez and rebuild the synthetic Tijuana from the other six cartels. The 2009–2018 gap (published: −4.47) will…

stay about −4.5
shrink toward zero but stay negative
turn positive: Tijuana more violent than its synthetic

Drop each cartel in turn. Without any of the other six, the gap stays between −4.8 and −3.5. Without Juárez it becomes +1.46: the synthetic Tijuana turns into mostly the Gulf Cartel, and the dip after 2009 disappears. The published effect is, in large part, Tijuana compared with Juárez's war years. This check is called leave-one-out. The paper describes its homicide result as robust to it; rerunning the authors' own specification and data, dropping Juárez reverses the sign.

4. When did the gap open?

The study's clearest result is about territory: after 2008 the Tijuana Cartel stayed in 21–56 municipalities a year, while its synthetic grew from 55 to 218. The authors read this as Enedina concentrating rather than expanding.

A backdating check pretends the change came earlier: fit the synthetic only on the years before a pretend start (say 2005), then look at 2005–2008. If Tijuana and its synthetic still track each other until 2008, the break lines up with Enedina. Try the start years on the left.

    The paper's story is less visible violence (homicides, narcomessages, political assassinations) and more hidden violence (disappearances) under a woman leader, plus a cartel that stopped expanding. Of the visible-violence claims, only narcomessages survives every check here, and it rests on three years of data before she took over. One case, eight cartels and a few years: synthetic control can suggest an effect here, but it can't settle one.

    6. Where else would you check?

    As a group: pick a claim from your own studies or life that a change worked, judged against a comparison (a new tutoring scheme at your school, judged against several similar schools, say, but find your own). Which one comparison, or which earlier trend, could be producing the difference? What would you drop, or move back in time, to find out?

    Points to compare
    • A good answer names the comparison that could carry the result on its own, as Juárez carried the homicide gap (step 3), and says what the claim looks like without it.
    • It asks when the difference started. A gap that opens before the change, like territory's in 2005–2008 (step 4), can't all be the change.
    • With few comparisons, coming out first proves little: first of 8 is still p = 0.125 (step 2).

    You should now be able to:

    • work out the smallest p-value a placebo test can give from the number of units compared (step 2);
    • use leave-one-out to tell whether a synthetic-control result rests on one donor (step 3);
    • use backdating to check whether a gap opened before the treatment (step 4).

    Assumptions
    • dataCartel-year data, 2000–2018, for the Tijuana Cartel and seven other major Mexican cartels (Sinaloa, Beltrán Leyva, Gulf, Zetas, Juárez, Milenio–Jalisco New Generation, Michoacán Family–Knights Templar), from the authors' replication package (Quintanilla Domínguez and Alcocer 2026).
    • interpretationAll estimates come from re-running the authors' synthetic-control specifications (R, Synth). Their script used four objects it never defined; the values used (Tijuana as treated, the seven others as donors, backdating to 2005–2007) follow the paper.
    • interpretationPlacebo p-value = Tijuana's rank among the 8 cartels by the ratio of post-2008 to pre-2008 root mean squared prediction error, divided by 8.
    • simplificationBackdating here ends both the fit period and the outcome predictor the year before each pretend start. Territory's pre-2009 gap with a 2005 start is about the same (−24 vs −25) under the authors' own version.
    • simplificationEach outcome has its own synthetic Tijuana, with its own weights.

    Left out: the paper's 44 case studies of women leaders worldwide and its comparison of male and female leaders' careers, which this page doesn't evaluate.
