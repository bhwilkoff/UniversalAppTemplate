    3. One fact, two types

    The data record schooling twice: educ in years, and nodegr, the same information cut at 12 years into diploma or not.

    As a group: you want to know whether the training helped men who left school early. Which column would you use, and why? What does the column you didn't pick show that yours hides?

    Points to compare
    • nodegr matches how employers often screen: a diploma is a credential. But it treats 8 years and 11 years as the same.
    • educ keeps that difference, but treating it as a number assumes every extra year counts the same. Any extra jump from finishing a diploma at year 12 doesn't show up as a step of its own.
    • You can make nodegr from educ, but not educ from nodegr. Cutting a quantity into categories throws information away, so the type is partly a choice, made when a variable is recorded or recoded.
    • Did your group say who “left school early” means? That definition decides which column fits.

    5. What R's label means, then a new column

    R calls treat, educ and re78 all “integer”. What does that tell you about their types?

    Nothing much: R's class says how the numbers are stored, not what they measure.
    They are all quantitative discrete: whole numbers.
    None of them is nominal, or R would have said so.

    Then: job satisfaction

    As a group: what type is jobsat, and what can you safely report?

    Ordinal: report the median and the share giving each answer. A mean assumes the steps are equal.
    Discrete: the codes are whole numbers, so the mean is as good as for educ.
    Nominal: the answers are categories, so only counts make sense, not a median.
    Continuous: satisfaction is a smooth feeling, so any summary works.

    6. Before you take a mean

    As a group: a colleague reports “mean region 2.6, mean score 3.6”. Before you trust either number, what would you check about each column, and how could each mean mislead a reader?

    Points to compare
    • region is probably four labels. If so, the mean of 2.6 means nothing, and renumbering the regions would change it. Report counts or shares.
    • score could be a count (points on a quiz: discrete) or a rating from 1 to 5 (ordinal). For a rating, the mean assumes equal steps; the median and the share giving each answer don't.
    • Checks: find out what each value stands for, count the different values, and look at a table of them. R's class won't tell you.
