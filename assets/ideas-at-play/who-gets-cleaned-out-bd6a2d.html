    What this exercise assumes
    • The toy datasets (X, Y and the canonical one) are invented for the exercise.
    • The larger population is simulated: 100 households of 1 to 6 people, mostly 2 to 4, 336 people in all, from a fixed seed.
    • Under individual randomization, each person is treated independently with probability p. Under household randomization, each household is treated with probability p and everyone in it shares that status.
    • Baseline income (thousand pesos per month) is simulated to fall by about 1.6 for each extra household member, plus noise. It plays no part in any cleaning rule; it is only used to check balance.
    • No outcomes, treatment effects or spillovers are simulated.

    Left out on purpose: estimating treatment effects, modelling spillovers, standard errors and power. R appears only as a display of the logic you design.
