    Assumption ledger and what is left out
    • Parameter (Breakout 1): 900 people, 3 start infected; each week an infectious person meets 4 people and infects each susceptible one with the chosen chance per meeting; a run stops when nobody is infectious or after 40 years (80 years for saved runs).
    • Parameter (Breakout 1): infectiousness 0.01% to 30%; duration 1 week to 40 years; recovery 0% to 100% per week once the duration has passed; treatment 0% to 5% per week.
    • Distribution (Breakout 1): random mixing by default; with Fixed partners each person meets the same 4 neighbours.
    • Simplification (Breakout 1): no deaths, births, ageing, risk groups or behaviour change; treated people stay counted as living with the virus but stop infecting others.
    • Data: the 0.7% and 25% HIV figures are rounded estimates of adult prevalence, used only as a rough reference.
    • Interpretation (Breakout 1): step 2 describes HIV in words and the student picks the dials; the settings are named only after the run. Step 4 accepts an end share from 0.7% (world average) to 25% (hardest-hit countries). The prediction verdict counts as right within 15 points. In step 5, two runs agree if they differ by at most 5 points at year 40 and use different settings.
    • Parameter (Breakout 2): 900 people on a 30 by 30 grid, 3 initial cases among the unvaccinated, contagious 4 days, 8 neighbours a day, chance per contact R0 / 32; R0 about 3 and about 8, mixed or clustered; each table row averages 4 seeded runs.
    • Simplification (Breakout 2): the vaccine works for everyone vaccinated, immunity never wanes, no age, travel or hospitals; 1 − 1/R0 assumes random mixing.
    • Interpretation: a plan passes when under 15% of all people are infected in every row; the step 2 prediction verdict uses the same 15% line and the same seeded simulations as the first table row, and counts as right within 10 points. The lessons from COVID-19 are limited to a more infectious variant and pockets of unvaccinated people.
