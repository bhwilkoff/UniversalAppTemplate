Assumption ledger and what is left out
  • Parameter: four recessive loss-of-function mutants, each fully Vul when homozygous; the screen puts them in 2 or 3 genes, seed 7.
  • Distribution: gene assignment and the dominant allele come from a seeded generator.
  • Simplification: complementation is all or none; reciprocal crosses give the same result; self progeny are ignored.
  • Simplification: the dominant allele gives Vul in every heterozygote, which breaks the test's assumption that all alleles are recessive. Step 4 asks for a prediction first, then lets you switch it on once all six crosses are run; it switches off when you leave step 4. Step 4 shows the six crosses as buttons and draws the genes on the chromosomes for the chosen cross; step 2 shows no chromosomes.
  • Interpretation: a step opens when the one before it is done: prediction revealed by the platform, six crosses run, a correct grouping checked, the dominant allele tried and the question interacted with. The Continue to step 5 button appears after your first interaction with the step 4 question (or after 20 seconds); read the feedback, then press it. Opened steps stay open.
  • Interpretation: the step 1 prediction and the written explanation refer to the screen shown when they were made. The prediction is a belief probe, so it has no right answer to count. It is compared with the completed table in step 2.
  • Data: none; results are computed from the hidden assignment.
  • Interpretation: gene names and chromosome positions are illustrative labels.

Left out: molecular mapping and cloning, intragenic and partial complementation, epistasis, maternal-effect and temperature-sensitive alleles, and screen saturation.
