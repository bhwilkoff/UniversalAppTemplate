Part 1: The farmers and the data

One hundred farmers live in a district: 50 are rich and 50 are poor. A new irrigation pump is available. Rich farmers own more land and hold reliable water rights, so the pump raises their harvest by $6,000. Poor farmers farm small, rain-fed plots with no water rights, so the pump does nothing for their harvest.

Pumps are expensive. Most rich farmers bought one. Poor farmers got pumps through a subsidy lottery, which gave pumps to half of them.

Part 4: Where does the bias come from?

The naïve difference can be split exactly into two pieces:

naïve difference = ATT + [ mean of Y0 among treated − mean of Y0 among controls ]

The bracketed term is the selection bias. It compares the treated and control groups in the world where nobody has a pump. Compute it and check that the identity holds.

Q14 · Where does the coefficient come from?

Agree with your group on an answer to Q15 first. Then check it.

Regression gives each group a weight proportional to its size times the variance of treatment inside it. Few comparisons exist among the rich, nearly all of whom have a pump. The lottery made the poor group the most informative.

The name for this is a variance-weighted average of within-group effects. Weight on group g is ng · pg(1 − pg), divided by the sum over groups.
