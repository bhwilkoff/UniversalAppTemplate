Human-shaped software

Build with AI, and keep the human parts human.

Human-shaped software starts from a problem only people can solve, like learning something hard or watching a film with a friend. An AI agent can write most of the code, and you keep the parts that make the app worth having: you write down what you value, play with what comes back, and publish it so other people can play with it too.

Read the principles Why build this way

The idea

Some problems were never computer-shaped.

Nilay Patel calls it software brain: seeing the whole world as a set of databases, each one waiting for the right code to take control of it. AI has turned that habit up as far as it will go, because an agent can write the code and the words for almost anything you can describe. But, learning, community, friendship, and the people we love only happen when a person does the work, and a first draft that no person has wrestled with is not the start of that work.

Every piece of software is built on values, whether or not anyone wrote them down. Human-shaped software writes them down, so that the people who use it and the AI agents that help build it can both see what it is for.

Software brain

Ten thousand film summaries written by AI in an afternoon is efficient, and every one of them was written by something that never watched the film.

Human-shaped

Notes written by the people who watched and reviewed each film are slower, and sometimes need correcting, and yet they keep the people who actually loved those films in the story.

A web full of words no one wrote is worse for everyone who reads it, and an answer that sends no one on to the people who did the work leaves those people out. Humanity is inefficient, and that is the point. Learning is inefficient too, and we have great tools for making it work better, as long as our values decide where those tools belong.
